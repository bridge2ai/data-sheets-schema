# Superseded: the software sub-element's role rule (#1081, #1082)

These are the rubric10-semantic evaluations as they stood before Element 8
sub-element 4 was re-adjudicated on 2026-09-08. Each has a live counterpart at
the canonical path; nothing here is the only copy of anything.

**What changed in the instrument.** The sub-element declared
`Fields: software_and_tools`. That slot is not in the D4D schema and appears in
no record, so its named field could never be satisfied and every evaluator
improvised a substitute. Its applicability was stated three incompatible ways —
the conditions table gated it with sub-element 3 on a referenced code
repository, a paragraph added the day before released it, and the local
`Applies to` restored the gate — and several rescores recorded `match: false`
rather than resolve the contradiction. Its failure list named capture, hosting
and instrumentation, and a pipeline whose outputs are excluded, but said
nothing about software that packaged the release, produced its metadata, or
validated it, which left eight of the twenty-four records undecidable and
produced opposite scores on the same packaging evidence.

**What the rule now says.** Score 1 when the record names, by name, software
that produced or transformed the data being distributed. Score 0 whenever it
does not, with five roles named that do not answer the question however
prominently they are documented: capture and instrumentation, hosting and
serving, packaging and metadata production, validation and quality assessment,
and a pipeline the record itself says produced outputs that are not in this
release.

**What was re-adjudicated.** All 24 production replicates of both arms, one
sub-element each, from the records alone, with the rule quoted verbatim to the
adjudicator rather than read from the agent definition (#1077). Only the
evaluations whose score moved were rewritten; the full adjudication of all 24,
changed or not, is in `../readjudication_software_role.json`.

**Do not compare a score here with a live one.** They are scores from two
instruments.
