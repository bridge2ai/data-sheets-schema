Bridge2AI-Voice project goal
The Bridge2AI-Voice project seeks to create an ethically sourced flagship dataset to enable future research in artificial intelligence and support critical insights into the use of voice as a biomarker of health.
Data acquisition protocol primary objective
The Bridge2AI Voice Data Acquisition protocol states its primary objective as: to build a large multi-institutional database of human voices, speech and respiratory sounds that is ethically sourced, diverse, and linked to multimodal health biomarkers to fuel voice AI research.

Bridge2AI-Voice Pediatric Dataset v1.1.0, published on PhysioNet on May 1, 2026, contains derived audio features for 23,533 recordings collected from 300 participants aged 2-18 at the Hospital for Sick Children. The release contains data considered low risk, including derivations such as spectrograms, but not the original voice recordings; detailed demographic, clinical and validated questionnaire data are also made available. The dataset is organized into derived audio features (under features), phenotypic information acquired during data collection (under phenotype) and metadata for the recordings (under metadata). Raw audio is not part of the PhysioNet project and is handled through a separate, controlled access route.
(1) The PhysioNet page notes that the Bridge2AI-Voice Adult Dataset is also available on PhysioNet (https://physionet.org/content/b2ai-voice/); no passage in the bundle states what the relation between the two datasets is (for example a part, version or supplement relation), so no related_datasets entry is recorded. Large parts of the project documentation in the bundle (the healthsheet answers for the v2.0.0 dataset with 833 adult participants, the v3.0.0 de-identification methods, adult devices, compensation and timeframe) describe the adult Bridge2AI-Voice dataset and were not used for this record's own slots. (2) The project documentation states that the pediatric dataset v1.0 contained data from 300 participants; the v1.1 release notes state that no new participants were released in that minor update, which is consistent with the 300 participants stated for v1.1.0. (3) The PhysioNet corresponding-author contact is not in the bundle (the page states that you must be logged in to view the contact information). (4) The project documentation lists a lead investigator "Jennifer Sui, MD (Hospital for Sick Children)", while the PhysioNet author list contains "Jennifer Siu"; the sources do not establish that these name the same person, so no affiliation is attached to the creator entry. (5) Several statements in this record come from the umbrella Bridge2AI Voice Data Acquisition protocol (University of South Florida) rather than from the pediatric release itself; they are attributed to the protocol and kept in the protocol's own tense.

Lack of open pediatric voice datasets
Data collection in pediatric populations poses challenges such as privacy issues, consent processes and the need for developmentally appropriate tasks; these factors have contributed to the lack of publicly available pediatric data sets that enable machine learning for pediatric voice analysis.
Sparse pediatric voice and speech literature
The data acquisition protocol states that the literature is sparser in terms of pediatric voice and speech analysis, partly due to ethical concerns and challenges in data acquisition for this cohort.

Derived audio data features. Binary files are made available as Parquet, an open-source column-oriented data file format; each element contains the participant identifier, the recording session identifier, the task performed, the number of time frames and the tensor data for the feature. The folder also contains static features in a tab-separated file and audio quality metrics. Each data file is associated with a data dictionary file with the same file stem and a JSON suffix, whose keys are the column names of the associated data file and whose description value gives a one-sentence summary of each column.

Special Protections
  • Pediatric patients will be enrolled only through the pediatric sites and not at USF; data collection for pediatric cohorts will only be performed at the pediatric participating institutions.
  • Checklist HRP-416 for children under 21 has been completed and is appended to the protocol.
Assent Procedures
When children participate, parents or guardians will provide consent, and children will provide verbal assent where appropriate. Where young children cannot reasonably be asked to assent (due to limitations in understanding due to age and development) they will not be asked to do so, which typically holds for children under seven years of age.
Guardian Consent
  • Parental or participant consent was obtained prior to data collection and data sharing.
  • The protocol excludes pediatric participants who do not receive consent from a parent or guardian to voice and clinical de-identified data being uploaded to an open-sourced database.
  • Parental permission will be obtained from one or both parents; the protocol describes the research as minimal risk and not requiring permission from both parents or legal guardians.

Access Review Process
The project documentation states that the feature-only and raw audio datasets are available under distinct agreements appropriate for the sensitivity of their respective content, and describes these routes. Registered access (features-only data): register on PhysioNet and confirm identity, and sign the Bridge2AI-Voice Registered Access Agreement. Controlled access (raw audio data): complete the Data Access Request Form (DARF) and the Data Use Agreement, submit the application to the Data Access Compliance Office for review and, upon approval, ensure a Data Use and Transfer Agreement (DTUA) is signed by an authorized official at the requesting institution; the DTUA template states that it is effective only upon review and approval of the Data Access Compliance Office (DACO). The PhysioNet release asks users interested in raw audio access to reach out to the access committee at DACO@b2ai-voice.org.
Retention Details
The data acquisition protocol states that identifiable data and datasheets linking participant numbers to participant information will be kept in each institution on local cloud storage for a maximum of 10 years after study completion.
