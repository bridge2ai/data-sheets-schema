https://chorus4ai.org/
CHoRUS
CHoRUS Dataset
https://chorus4ai.org/
The CHoRUS (Patient-Focused Collaborative Hospital Repository Uniting Standards) dataset is a multicenter, multimodal, high-resolution clinical care dataset produced by the CHoRUS project, which the project website describes as one data generation project of four in the NIH-funded Bridge2AI consortium, "creating a patient-focused collaborative hospital repository uniting standards for clinical care AI" under the heading "Expanding Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning to Improve Recovery from Acute Illness". The project website reports the current released dataset as 50,000 patient admissions from ICU, PICU, and NICU, 1.6 billion rows of EHR OMOP data, 7,642 admissions with radiology data, and 23 Tb of waveform data; it lists the anticipated final dataset as 100,000 patient admissions, 9 different data modalities, and 14 data contributing hospitals, with 60+ CHoRUS consortium members across 20 different institutions. The data-type table of the AIM-AHEAD Bridge2AI for Clinical Care webinar (September 2025) lists demographics, medication administration (dosing time-stamped upon each infusion change or dose administration), procedures and diagnoses (documentation by providers), nursing flowsheets (high-frequency documentation), clinical notes, imaging, waveform telemetry, and waveform EEG; the same webinar states that imaging de-identification was in process for a larger cohort and that EEG extraction was in process. The NIH grant abstract states the project's plan to develop capabilities to acquire, standardize, tokenize, store, visualize, and label data such as structured electronic health record data, tokenized unstructured electronic health record data, telemetry and EEG waveforms, imaging, and social determinants of health, to ensure that data elements feature appropriate contextual factors such as geographic distance to the nearest hospital, and to develop the necessary network, tools, standards, data, and education to build machine-learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI)-derived models.
