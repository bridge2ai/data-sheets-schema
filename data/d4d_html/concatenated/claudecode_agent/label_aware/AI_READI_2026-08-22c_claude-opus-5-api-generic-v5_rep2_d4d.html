Response
To create and openly share a flagship, ethically sourced, multimodal dataset that enables future artificial intelligence and machine learning research into type 2 diabetes mellitus, including salutogenic pathways back to health.
To assemble a large multimodal dataset uniformly balanced for T2DM severity, self-reported race/ethnicity and sex, so that models trained on it are less subject to the imbalances of existing large datasets.
To permit downstream pseudo-time manifold analysis, reconstructing a temporal atlas of T2DM development and reversal toward health from cross-sectional data collected across a spectrum of disease severity, agnostic to existing classification criteria.
To establish and disseminate standards, best practices and guidelines for the collection, preparation and sharing of AI-ready biomedical data, as a blueprint that future data generation projects can follow.

Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Version 3.0.0 of the flagship multimodal dataset generated by AI-READI (the Bridge2AI Salutogenesis Grand Challenge), containing cross-sectional data from 2,280 adult participants with and without type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) collected between 19 July 2023 and 1 May 2025 at three United States sites (University of Alabama at Birmingham, University of California San Diego, University of Washington) and harmonized across sites. Recruitment sampling aimed at an approximately equal distribution of participants across diabetes severity groups (no diabetes; pre-diabetes or lifestyle-controlled; controlled by oral or non-insulin injectable medication; insulin-controlled), self-reported race/ethnicity, and sex, so that the data would support downstream artificial intelligence and machine learning analyses that existing claims or electronic health record sources cannot. Modalities include survey and clinical data mapped to the OMOP Common Data Model, blood and urine laboratory results, physical and vision measurements, cognitive (MoCA) and monofilament testing, 12-lead electrocardiography stored as WFDB, retinal photography, optical coherence tomography, optical coherence tomography angiography and fluorescence lifetime imaging ophthalmoscopy stored as DICOM, ten-day continuous glucose monitoring and wearable activity monitoring stored as Open mHealth, and ten-day home environmental sensor data. The release contains no protected health information; information on sex, race/ethnicity and medications used has also been removed and is held only under controlled access. The dataset comprises 356,343 files and approximately 3.82 TB, organized according to the Clinical Dataset Structure (CDS) v0.1.1 with one directory per datatype, then modality, then device, then participant.

Response
The lack of well-designed, high quality, large and inclusive multimodal datasets has limited the ability to understand and affect the course of complex, multi-organ diseases such as type 2 diabetes mellitus.
A major barrier to AI in health research is the absence of datasets that are AI-ready, that is, immediately usable for AI/ML research without reformatting and preprocessing.
A meta-epidemiological review of type 2 diabetes studies published between 2000 and 2020 found that samples lacked racial and ethnic diversity; the dataset is designed as a step toward addressing that inequity.
Current understanding of salutogenesis in type 2 diabetes, that is, the pathway from disease back to health, is limited.

Mechanism DetailsNotes
REDCap was used for the online recruitment interface, electronic consent, participant-reported questionnaires and for entry of clinical data from the visit including the medications assessment, vitals, visual acuity, contrast sensitivity, monofilament results and complete blood count results. Range checks in REDCap flagged values outside expected minima and maxima.
Retinal imaging used six devices chosen for their different but partially overlapping modalities: Optomed Aurora IQ handheld fundus camera (undilated), iCare EIDON widefield TrueColor confocal fundus system, Heidelberg Spectralis HRA OCT/OCTA, Topcon Maestro2 3D OCT-1, Topcon Triton DRI OCT, Carl Zeiss Meditec Cirrus 5000, and Heidelberg fluorescence lifetime imaging ophthalmoscopy. Both eyes were imaged, under dilation except for the Optomed.
Topcon, Optomed, iCare and Carl Zeiss loaned devices for research at no cost, and Heidelberg Engineering, Dexcom and Garmin provided research discounts on study devices.
A 12-lead electrocardiogram was recorded with a Philips PageWriter TC30 Cardiograph while the participant sat reclined or lay supine, with the chair position recorded relative to supine (0, 30, 60 or 90 degrees).
A Dexcom G6 real-time integrated continuous glucose monitoring system was worn for ten continuous days, recording glucose every five minutes. Participants were masked to the results during the monitoring period.
A Garmin Vivosmart 5 fitness tracker was worn on the wrist for ten continuous days at a five-second sampling frequency; participants recorded which wrist was used and their dominant hand on a returned data sheet.
A custom-designed environmental sensor unit (LeeLab Anura, Karalis Johnson Retina Center, University of Washington) was placed in the home for ten days, recording particulate matter counts (PM1.0, PM2.5, PM4, PM10), temperature, relative humidity, volatile organic compounds, nitrogen oxides and eleven multi-spectral light intensity measurements. Participants noted where in the home it was placed.
The environmental sensor and the fitness tracker were chosen with participant privacy as a primary concern: they capture no video or audio, have no GPS location monitoring, and are not synced with participant-owned devices.
Blood (53 mL, non-fasting) and urine were collected during the visit. CLIA-certified laboratories local to each site performed complete blood counts on fresh whole blood; all other assays were performed by the University of Washington Nutrition and Obesity Research Center on stored frozen samples batch-shipped on dry ice approximately quarterly.
Vision and neurological testing instruments: M&S Technologies Electronic Visual Acuity tester with a 2.0 neutral density filter for mesopic conditions, Mars Perceptrix letter contrast sensitivity chart, Topcon KR 800 autorefractor, NIDEK and Topcon lensometers, a 10 g monofilament applied at three plantar locations per foot, and the Montreal Cognitive Assessment administered on an iPad using the MoCA Duo application.
Blood pressure and heart rate were measured twice, two minutes apart, with an automatic oscillometric medically approved device; devices in use across sites included the OMRON HEM 907XL, the Medline MDS4001 and the Welch Allyn 6000 series vital signs monitor.

    Source Data
    All patients aged 40 years or older who had a medical encounter within each health system site (University of Alabama at Birmingham, University of California San Diego, University of Washington). Patients with T2DM and pre-diabetes were identified by screening electronic health records for ICD-10 codes E11.X and R73.09 respectively; patients without diabetes have no encounters with these codes.
    Strategies
    Non-probability, triple-balanced sampling. Rather than targeting the demographic distribution of the US population, the study recruits four race/ethnic groups (Asian, Black, Hispanic, White), four T2DM categories (no diabetes; pre-diabetes/lifestyle-controlled; controlled by oral or non-insulin injectable medication; insulin-controlled) and both biological sexes in approximately equal proportions. Participants are recruited in waves; the composition and size of each wave is influenced by the observed participation characteristics of race/ethnicity, gender, T2DM severity and site, and is adjusted by under- and oversampling groups as needed. Contact pools are mailed a personalized hardcopy invitation letter and an invitation email directing them into an online REDCap recruitment interface.
    Why Not Representative
    The sample is deliberately balanced rather than demographically representative, so that machine learning models trained on it are not skewed by the population distribution. Recruitment is predominantly urban and hospital-based, may not capture the full range of cultural and socioeconomic backgrounds, and does not include other races and ethnicities such as Pacific Islanders and Native Americans. Participation is voluntary, introducing volunteer bias, and participants must speak and read English.

  • Raw Data Details
    The raw data are saved and are expected to be preserved by the project for at least the duration of the project, but are not currently anticipated to be shared outside the project team, because they have not been mapped to standardized terminologies and because raw records may accidentally contain personal health or personally identifiable information in free-text fields. Raw data may be included in future releases of the controlled-access dataset.
Preprocessing Details
Data were mapped to standardized terminologies and models wherever possible: the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership Common Data Model for clinical data, DICOM for medical imaging, the WaveForm DataBase standard for electrocardiography, Open mHealth for wearable activity and continuous glucose monitoring, and the NASA ASCII file format guidelines for environmental sensor data.
Proprietary device outputs were converted before upload: Topcon .fda files and Heidelberg FLIO .sdt files were converted to DICOM, Garmin .FIT files were converted to the Open mHealth standard, and ECG exports were placed in WFDB form.
Protected health information was withheld from the release, and information related to sex, race/ethnicity and medications used was additionally removed, in the latter case to prevent stigmatization of findings. Five-digit zip code, genetic sequencing data, past health records and traffic and accident reports are held under controlled access rather than released publicly.
The dataset was organized according to the Clinical Dataset Structure v0.1.1, with one directory per datatype at the root, then modality, then device, then participant, and with manifest and description metadata files generated at each level.
Processing for this release used a combination of automated pipelines and custom steps; the changelog records processing as ad hoc and custom for version 1.0.0 and as automated plus custom for the year-two and year-three data included in this version.
Cleaning Details
Quality control at the point of data entry: clinical data outside expected minimum and maximum ranges were flagged in REDCap and appeared in reports viewed by clinical research coordinators and data managers, who then examined the participant record and determined whether an error was likely.
Records were checked for credibility against prespecified reasonable ranges, for incorrect flow through prescribed skip patterns, for missing data that could be filled directly from other portions of an individual's record, and for the omission or duplication of records.
Editing was done only under the guidance and approval of the site principal investigator. Where corrected data were available elsewhere in the respondent's answers the error was corrected; where there was no logical or appropriate way to correct a value, the site principal investigator reviewed it and decided whether it should be removed.
