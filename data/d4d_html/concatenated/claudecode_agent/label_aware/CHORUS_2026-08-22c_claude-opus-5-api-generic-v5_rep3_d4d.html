Motivation

For what purpose was the dataset created?

Response
To generate an AI-ready critical care dataset for machine learning and artificial intelligence applications aimed at characterizing acute and critical care illness, predicting complications, and measuring treatment response among patients with acute or critical illness.
To develop the most diverse, high-resolution, ethically sourced, AI-ready dataset to answer the grand challenge of improving recovery from acute illness.

Collection Process

How was the data associated with each instance acquired?

CHoRUS Dataset
Patient-Focused Collaborative Hospital Repository Uniting Standards (CHoRUS) for Equitable AI
CHoRUS (Patient-Focused Collaborative Hospital Repository Uniting Standards) is a multicenter, multimodal, high-resolution critical care dataset assembled by one of the four data generation projects of the NIH Common Fund Bridge2AI program. It brings together retrospectively collected clinical data from intensive care admissions at 14 contributing hospitals within a collaboration spanning 20 academic centers, standardized so that structured electronic health record data follow the OMOP Common Data Model, imaging follows DICOM, telemetry waveforms follow WFDB, EEG follows EDF+ and Persyst formats, and clinical notes are extracted and tokenized with the OHNLP toolkit. The currently released dataset is reported as 50,000 patient admissions from ICU, PICU, and NICU settings, 1.6 billion rows of OMOP electronic health record data, 7,642 admissions with radiology data, and 23 Tb of waveform data; the anticipated final dataset covers 100,000 patient admissions across nine data modalities. All nine modalities are held under controlled access. OMOP and telemetry data reside in a cloud enclave, while full clinical note text remains stored locally at contributing sites and only tokens are shared. A holdout test set is sequestered so that models can be externally validated, with the aim of aiding marketplace adoption of AI models for acute and critical care.
Released under controlled access while assembly continues toward the larger anticipated final dataset.

Preprocessing Details
Standardization of acquired clinical data to the OMOP Common Data Model, using ongoing and new standardization practices and validated semantic mappings that connect source formats to international standards.
Extraction and tokenization of unstructured electronic health record text using the OHNLP toolkit, so that tokens rather than full note text are shared.
Transformation of data using approaches that limit re-identification, together with tokenization of identifiers.
De-identification of imaging drawn from PACS, in process for the larger imaging cohort at the time of reporting.
Conversion and preparation of waveform data using the CHoRUS waveform conversion scripts and documentation.
Geocoding of OMOP Location entities via DeGauss to supply contextual factors such as geographic distance to the nearest hospital and social determinants of health.
Cleaning Details
Characterization reports are produced from each site data submission and returned to the contributing site, supporting review and correction of the submitted extract.
Data contribution follows standard operating procedures that have undergone internal validation and review within the CHoRUS network, and submission issues are raised in context-specific Standards and Data Acquisition discussion forums for resolution.
  • Labeling Details
    A visualization and annotation environment is developed to label data with targets important for prediction; the project also builds capabilities across the multi-center network to acquire, standardize, tokenize, store, visualize, and label data.
