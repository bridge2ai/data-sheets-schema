CM4AI seeks to map the spatiotemporal architecture of human cells and use these maps toward the grand challenge of interpretable genotype-phenotype learning, through a coordinated effort involving three complementary mapping approaches: proteomic mass spectrometry, cellular imaging, and genetic perturbation via CRISPR/Cas9.

CM4AI's objective is to deliver machine-readable hierarchical maps of cell architecture as AI-ready data produced from multimodal interrogation of 100 chromatin modifiers and 100 metabolic enzymes involved in cancer, neuropsychiatric and cardiac disorders in disease-relevant cell lines under perturbed and unperturbed conditions, together with quarterly data releases of the map-input data streams.

CM4AI's overarching mission is to produce ethical, AI-ready datasets of cell architecture, inferred from multimodal data collected for human cell lines, to enable transformative biomedical AI research. CM4AI defines AI-ready biomedical data as fully characterized FAIR data of known provenance that can be ethically and reliably processed by AI applications, whose models and software are available and well-described for validation and reuse, and whose predictions may be fully explained and interpreted to the user as needed.

CM4AI
Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence
CM4AI is the Functional Genomics Data Generation Project of the NIH Bridge2AI program. It delivers machine-readable hierarchical maps of cell architecture as AI-ready data, together with quarterly data releases of the map-input data streams. The released data are produced by multimodal interrogation of chromatin modifiers, metabolic enzymes and other proteins involved in cancer, neuropsychiatric and cardiac disorders, in disease-relevant human cell lines under treated and untreated conditions. Two cell lines are used: MDA-MB-468 triple negative breast cancer cells, untreated or treated with paclitaxel or vorinostat, and KOLF2.1J induced pluripotent stem cells, undifferentiated or differentiated into neural progenitor cells, neurons and cardiomyocytes. Three measurement modalities contribute: mass spectrometry based proteomics, comprising affinity purification mass spectrometry (AP-MS) and size exclusion chromatography mass spectrometry (SEC-MS); spatial proteomics by immunofluorescence confocal imaging; and genetic perturbation by CRISPR/Cas9 single-cell screens. Output data are packaged with provenance graphs and rich metadata as AI-ready datasets in RO-Crate format using the FAIRSCAPE framework, and are deposited in LibraData, the University of Virginia Dataverse. CM4AI reports a total data volume of 21.4 TB. Four DOI-identified releases are documented in the sources for this record and are enumerated as resources: March 2025, June 2025, October 2025 and the current June 2026 release. CM4AI is a collaboration of UCSD, UCSF, Stanford, UVA, Yale, UT Austin, UA Birmingham, Simon Fraser University, and the Hastings Center.

SEC-MS is used for proteome-wide complex and interaction mapping as an orthogonal mass spectrometry approach to AP-MS, and is performed in the Nevan Krogan laboratory at the University of California San Francisco. As reported in the May 2024 CM4AI project preprint, SEC-MS on MDA-MB-468 cells under three conditions identified 72 of 100 chromatin modifiers, 52 of them integral components of protein complexes, and detected interaction profiles of over a thousand complexes with thousands of proteins exhibiting differential elution profiles between control and paclitaxel- or vorinostat-treated cells. SEC-MS in KOLF2.1J iPSCs and derivatives uncovered more than 700 protein complexes in both parental iPSCs and differentiated neurons.

Spatial subcellular organization is mapped by immunofluorescence-based staining (ICC-IF) and confocal microscopy in the Lundberg Lab at Stanford University, using the Human Protein Atlas resource of antibodies. As reported in the May 2024 CM4AI project preprint, spatial proteomics mapping of 100 chromatin regulators in MDA-MB-468 cells with and without paclitaxel or vorinostat was complete at that time, with another 500 proteins pending significant hits from the genetic perturbation and protein interaction results.

https://cm4ai.org/#preprocessing-fairscape-packagingRO-Crate packaging and provenance graph computation with FAIRSCAPE
CM4AI output data are packaged with provenance graphs and rich metadata as AI-ready datasets in RO-Crate format using an extended, client-server version of the FAIRSCAPE framework. The client-side package, FAIRSCAPE-CLI, is called when a computation or coherent set of computations in the pipeline completes and is passed metadata defining JSON-Schema descriptions for the datasets in the computational unit, together with the inputs, computations, software, models and outputs; it creates an RO-Crate package holding the datasets, metadata and software, or resolvable references to them, and unique stubs for identifier creation. The RO-Crates are sent to the FAIRSCAPE server, where they are registered and assigned persistent, resolvable, globally unique identifiers using the ARK scheme, then decomposed into their individual components, which are also registered and assigned identifiers. Finally the server computes end-to-end entailments on each RO-Crate's provenance as expressed in the EVI Evidence Graph Ontology and links them together where possible. Identifiers resolve to machine- and human-readable landing pages expressed in JSON-LD using vocabularies from Schema.org, EVI and other public ontologies, with alternate serializations in JSON-LD and RDF-XML.

The maintenance plan in the release records states that the dataset will be regularly updated and augmented through the end of the project in November 2026, with updates on a quarterly basis, and with long term preservation in the University of Virginia Dataverse supported by committed institutional funds.
