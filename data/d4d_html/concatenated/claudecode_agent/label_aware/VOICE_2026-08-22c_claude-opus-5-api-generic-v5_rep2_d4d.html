ResponseSource Caveats
To generate a large-scale, multi-institutional, ethically sourced and diverse voice, speech and cough database linked to multimodal health biomarkers, in order to fuel voice AI research.
To build predictive models that assist in the screening, diagnosis and treatment of a broad range of diseases, and to integrate the use of voice as a biomarker of health into clinical care.
To enable the development, benchmarking or validation of clinically applicable machine-learning models for diagnosing health conditions using voice data, including vocal pathologies and neurological, psychiatric, respiratory and pediatric voice disorders.
To introduce the field of acoustic biomarkers by developing new standards of acoustic and voice data collection and analysis for voice AI research, and to create interoperable bioinformatics standards for voice data.
To create a flagship, standardized and ethically sourced dataset of 10,000 voices linked to health information, with an anticipated enrollment count of 10,000 by 2027.
The project documentation gives a target of 10,000 voices and an anticipated enrollment of 10,000 by 2027; the IRB protocol states a target of 30,000 participants and the audiomics white paper states a primary deliverable of a publicly available database of 30,000 human voices. All three are tier 2 sources, so the ranking does not decide between them and both figures are recorded.
To define ethical and legal norms safeguarding participants' data while supporting data-sharing efforts, and to develop infrastructure for audiomics data storage and sharing within and across research institutions.

Collection Process

How was the data associated with each instance acquired?

doi:10.13026/8xbn-nq66
Bridge2AI-Voice
Bridge2AI-Voice: An ethically-sourced, diverse voice dataset linked to health information
Bridge2AI-Voice is a large, ethically sourced, demographically diverse voice dataset linked to health information, generated by the Bridge2AI-Voice consortium under the NIH Common Fund Bridge2AI program. This record describes the adult flagship release, version 3.1.0, published on PhysioNet. The release contains derived audio features for 833 adult participants recruited at five sites in North America, selected on the basis of conditions known to manifest in the voice waveform: voice disorders, neurological and neurodegenerative disorders, mood and psychiatric disorders, and respiratory disorders, together with control participants without those conditions. Data were collected in clinic on study iPads using a custom application (the Bridge2AI-Voice app) that administers standardized acoustic tasks (breathing, cough, sustained vowels, glides, loudness, diadochokinesis, validated reading passages, picture description, story recall, free speech, and cognitive-linguistic tasks), demographic and confounder surveys, and validated patient-reported outcome questionnaires. Clinical diagnoses were validated by qualified clinicians against per-condition gold standards such as laryngoscopy and stroboscopy, spirometry and flow-volume loops, brain CT and MRI, and electronic health record review. The published release contains data considered low risk. Raw audio waveforms are not included; instead the features folder distributes spectrograms, mel spectrograms, mel-frequency cepstral coefficients, pitch contours, Speech Articulatory Coding estimates of electromagnetic articulography, loudness and periodicity, phonetic posteriorgrams, static acoustic features from openSMILE, Praat and Parselmouth, and audio quality metrics. A phenotype folder distributes tab-delimited demographic, confounder, enrollment, diagnosis, questionnaire and task tables with accompanying JSON data dictionaries, and a metadata folder distributes per-recording metadata including task instructions, prompts, microphone and gain information. Raw audio is disseminated separately under a controlled access mechanism via Synapse to protect participant privacy, because voice is inherently re-identifying.

Response
Previous studies on machine learning-based voice diagnosis produced promising results, but their sample sizes were too small or they lacked the key metadata needed for training robust, clinically useful models. Current literature has been studied only on small- to medium-sized datasets with limited data outside acoustic measures and not linked to multi-modal health data.
Comparing studies and pooling data is challenging because of a lack of existing standards for how voice and other acoustics are measured and collected.
Within industry, voice datasets are often private, limiting the ability to audit machine learning models and the accuracy of training data labels, and preventing external auditing of training datasets to ensure fair representation of diverse populations.
There is a lack of prospective study and validation of AI algorithms in audiomics on external datasets, compounded by the limited size, quality and diversity of existing open-source datasets.
Pediatric voice and speech data are sparse because of ethical concerns and challenges in data acquisition for that cohort.

Mechanism DetailsNotes
Adult data were collected in clinic using a custom software application, the Bridge2AI-Voice app, running on a study tablet, with the assistance of a trained research assistant. Participants wore a head-mounted headset with a microphone where possible.
Hardware consisted of iPads (9th or 10th generation) and iPad Air (5th generation) with an Avid AE-36 microphone connected to the iPad through an Apple dongle. Distinct iPad devices were used at each site.
Demographic, confounder, diagnostic and validated questionnaire data were captured through the application and managed in REDCap. The supporting REDCap data dictionary, metadata and instrument PDFs are published at https://github.com/eipm/bridge2ai-redcap.
A Bridge2AI Voice Web app and a Bridge2AI Voice iOS app support remote data collection. No software is downloaded or installed for the web app, no login is required, data are held only during the collection phase and removed when the page is closed, and data are transmitted over a secure HTTPS protocol to a HIPAA-compliant storage server hosted by an NIH STRIDES partner.
Remote data collection had not occurred for the initial dataset release; data in the published releases were collected in clinic.

Different sites have different collection configurations, and the collection protocol changed over the course of the study. Distinct iPad devices were used at each site.

Deidentification Details
All direct identifiers were removed. Indirect identifiers were removed where they created a significant risk of participant re-identification through combination with other public data available on social media, in government registries or elsewhere; these include select geographic or demographic identifiers and some information about household composition or cultural identity. Non-identifying elements revealing highly sensitive information, such as household income, mental health status and traumatic life experiences, were also removed. Country of data collection was retained while state and province were removed. Raw audio transcripts were reviewed and any audio recordings containing potentially identifying information or external voices were removed from the release. The dataset applies the HIPAA de-identification rules, rebases or replaces dates by integers, generalizes or removes geographic information and removes narrative text fields, but does not achieve K-anonymization at k greater than or equal to 2.

Preprocessing Details
Raw audio was converted to monaural and resampled to 16 kHz with a Butterworth anti-aliasing filter applied, producing the standardized audio from which all derived features were computed.
Spectrograms were computed with the short-time Fast Fourier Transform using a 25 ms window size, a 10 ms hop length and a 400-point FFT, then downsampled by a factor of two in the time domain. Dimension is 201 by T.
Mel spectrograms of 60 mel bins and 60 mel-frequency cepstral coefficients were computed with the same window, hop and FFT parameters as the spectrograms. Each is of dimension 60 by T.
Articulatory features were generated with the Speech Articulatory Coding (sparc) package at 50 Hz, yielding kinematic traces of six vocal tract articulators — tongue dorsum, tongue body, tongue tip, lower incisor, upper lip and lower lip — as X and Y positions, together with loudness, periodicity and pitch measures. Loudness and periodicity are estimated using 20 ms windows.
Phonetic posteriorgrams, a time-varying categorical distribution over 40 acoustic units of speech, were generated with the ppgs package at 100 Hz.
Static acoustic features capturing temporal dynamics and acoustic characteristics were extracted with openSMILE, and phonetic and prosodic features providing measures of fundamental frequency, formants and voice quality were computed with Parselmouth and Praat. Pitch contours were computed with torchaudio over a range of 80 to 500 Hz, and with sparc over a range of 50 to 550 Hz.
Transcriptions were generated using OpenAI's Whisper Large model.
Raw audio files and the questionnaire data retrieved from ReproSchema-UI or exported from REDCap were converted to be compliant with the Brain Imaging Data Structure version 1.9.0.
All processing for the release was performed with the open-source b2aiprep library version 3.0.0, which organizes and preprocesses the dataset, and senselab, which processes audio files for research tasks.
Features are stored in two formats: a fixed feature format holding static features extracted from the entire waveform, and a temporal format whose length varies per audio file according to the length of the recording.
Cleaning Details
No pre-processing for cleaning the data was performed; the healthsheet records that the data were not cleaned.
An audit protocol is applied instead of cleaning: missingness tables are generated, distributions and outliers are checked, categorical responses are checked against the schema, and audio tasks are run through quality control metrics. For waveforms the checks cover the amount of silence and the duration; for speech, the produced speech is checked against the intended passage.
No instances were excluded from the dataset at the time of preprocessing on the basis of participant characteristics; the healthsheet records that no instances, including those relating to participants under 18, were discarded.

    Tool Descriptions
    openSMILE, the Speech and Music Interpretation by Large-space Extraction toolkit, was used to extract acoustic features capturing temporal dynamics and acoustic characteristics. Praat and its Python interface Parselmouth were used to compute phonetic and prosodic features including fundamental frequency, formants and voice quality measures. torchaudio was used to compute spectrograms, mel spectrograms, mel-frequency cepstral coefficients and pitch contours. The Speech Articulatory Coding package was used to estimate electromagnetic articulography traces for six articulators together with loudness, periodicity and pitch at 50 Hz. The ppgs package was used to generate high-fidelity neural phonetic posteriorgrams over 40 phoneme categories at 100 Hz. OpenAI's Whisper Large model was used to generate transcriptions of the audio. b2aiprep, the open-source Bridge2AI data processing library, was used to organize and preprocess the dataset and to merge the source data into the phenotype files; senselab was used to process audio files for research tasks.
    Tool Accuracy
    • Certain aspects of the data, for example transcription, were generated using off-the-shelf models that have not been audited for correctness.
  • Analysis Method
    Gold standard clinical labeling with a single labeler per instance; no inter-annotator agreement analysis was performed because only one label was provided per instance.
    Disagreement Patterns
    • Not assessed. Variations in diagnostic practices between sites and clinicians are recorded as a confounding factor rather than measured as annotator disagreement.
    Annotation Quality Details
    Human-level performance in the applications the dataset is intended to address varies widely. Labeling followed the Bridge2AI protocols and ICD-10 codes with per-condition gold standard validation methods.
  • Missing Data Patterns
    Optional protocol questions left unanswered by participants; cohort-specific branching, in which each participant answered a common set of questions and then only the additional questions relevant to their primary diagnostic category; phenotype data files contain a row for a participant only if at least one column is not missing; feature parquet files omit recordings for which the corresponding feature could not be generated, so per-feature record counts differ; spectrograms, mel spectrograms, mel-frequency cepstral coefficients, phonetic posteriorgrams and electromagnetic articulography estimates are absent for sensitive records, audio checks and free speech tasks.
    Missing Data Causes
    Questions that are optional in the protocol; data collection irregularities during the session; cohort-dependent branching of the instrument; feature extraction failures caused by recording length or other issues; deliberate removal during de-identification.
    Handling Strategy
    A missingness table generated during the internal audit is included with the dataset. No imputation was performed; missing values are left missing.
