Motivation

For what purpose was the dataset created?

NameResponse
Flagship ethically sourced voice dataset linked to health information
To create a flagship, standardized and ethically sourced dataset of voices linked to health information, so that voice can be used as a biomarker of health in clinical care and so that voice AI research has a large, multi-institutional and diverse resource linked to multimodal health biomarkers.
Fill the gap in large, well-documented clinical voice data
To address a gap in the availability of large-scale, diverse and well-documented voice data for clinical machine-learning applications, where previous studies produced promising results but used sample sizes that were too small or lacked the key metadata needed for training robust, clinically useful models.
Support development, benchmarking and validation of clinical AI models
To provide a resource from which clinically applicable AI and machine-learning models can be developed, benchmarked or validated for the diagnosis of a wide range of health conditions from voice, including vocal pathologies and neurological, psychiatric and respiratory disorders.
Establish standards for audiomics data acquisition and AI readiness
To introduce the field of acoustic biomarkers by defining standards for audiomics data acquisition, interoperability and AI readiness, so that voice and speech data collected by different groups can be compared and pooled.

doi:10.13026/37yb-1t42
Bridge2AI-Voice
Bridge2AI-Voice: An ethically-sourced, diverse voice dataset linked to health information
The adult Bridge2AI-Voice dataset is an ethically sourced, demographically diverse collection of data derived from voice, speech and respiratory-sound recordings, linked to clinical diagnoses, validated questionnaire responses and demographic metadata. It is produced by the Voice project, a Precision Public Health grand challenge funded by the NIH Common Fund Bridge2AI program. Release 3.1.0, published on PhysioNet on 1 May 2026, contains data for 833 adult participants collected at five sites in North America. Participants were selected on the basis of known conditions that manifest in the voice waveform, across voice disorders, neurological and neurodegenerative disorders, mood and psychiatric disorders and respiratory disorders, together with control participants without those conditions. The public release carries data considered low risk: derived representations of the audio such as spectrograms, mel spectrograms, MFCCs, articulatory and phonetic features, together with phenotype tables organized as demographics, confounders, per-condition diagnosis files, enrollment records, questionnaires and per-task metadata. The original voice recordings are not part of this release; raw audio is distributed separately under a controlled access mechanism.

NameResponse
Small sample sizes in prior voice machine-learning studies
Currently published AI and machine-learning models linking voice to the disease categories of interest use datasets ranging from 20 to 400 patients and report high diagnostic accuracies that do not generalize. In machine-learning applications for screening and diagnosis of laryngeal cancer, most studies reported training datasets of fewer than 300 patients and only two studies used more than one data modality.
Absence of standards for voice and speech data collection
Comparing studies and pooling data is challenging because of the lack of standards in how voice and speech data are collected and measured, which precludes meta-analysis and limits external validity.
Industry voice data lacks clinical annotation and cannot be audited
Industry holds vast amounts of voice data from users but lacks accurate clinical data or demographic information, which limits the ability to annotate data for AI training, to compare algorithmic outputs with criterion standard medical assessment, and to audit training datasets externally for fair representation of diverse populations.
Need for publicly available voice databases independent of the private sector
As voice biomarkers continue to emerge it is crucial for the research community to have access to publicly available voice databases without reliance on the private sector, and to prospective validation of AI algorithms on external datasets.

    Acquisition Details
    The data were directly observable and reported by subjects. Voice samples were collected prospectively using a custom application on a tablet, with a headset used where possible, following the Bridge2AI-Voice protocols. Demographic information, health questionnaires, targeted questionnaires on known confounders for voice, and disease-specific information were collected in the same session. Clinical diagnoses were verified by clinicians reviewing audio or imaging data, looking at electronic health records, or checking medication prescriptions. Mechanism DetailsName
Voice, breathing, cough and speech data are recorded with the Bridge2AI-Voice application, a smartphone and tablet application hosting the data acquisition protocols. A total of 22 acoustic tasks are recorded through the application. Data are collected on iPads (9th or 10th generation) and iPad Air (5th generation) using an Avid AE-36 microphone and an Apple dongle to connect it to the iPad.     All participants have been duly informed and agreed to the collection and use of their data through prospective informed consent.   1. Name
    Missing data in the released tables
    Missing Data Patterns
    Phenotype data files contain rows for a participant only where at least one column is not missing. Each individual answered a common set of questions and then responded to additional questions relevant to their primary diagnostic category, so items outside a participant's category are systematically absent rather than missing at random. Feature files omit recordings for which a given feature could not be generated, and omit sensitive records and audio checks.
    Missing Data Causes
    Some questionnaire items are optional; there may be data collection irregularities that caused information to be missing from individual instances; features could not be generated for some files because of length or other issues; and features derived from free-speech audio, together with data elements encoded as sensitive in the REDCap data dictionary, were removed before release.
    Handling Strategy
    Missing values are retained as missing and documented. The dataset has been audited internally for missingness and consistency by the data release team, and a missingness table is included with the dataset.

Audio standardization
Raw audio was preprocessed by converting to monaural and resampling to 16 kHz, with a Butterworth anti-aliasing filter applied.
Derivation of acoustic and articulatory features
From the standardized audio, spectrograms were computed with the short-time fast Fourier transform using a 25 ms window, a 10 ms hop length and a 400-point FFT, then downsampled by a factor of two in the time domain; 60 mel-frequency cepstral coefficients were extracted from those spectrograms; mel spectrograms were computed with the same parameters and 60 mel bins; articulatory features were generated with the Speech Articulatory Coding package, giving kinematic traces of vocal tract articulators and source features together with loudness, periodicity and pitch measures at 50 Hz; acoustic features capturing temporal dynamics were extracted with openSMILE; phonetic and prosodic measures of fundamental frequency, formants and voice quality were computed with Parselmouth and Praat; and phonetic posteriorgrams over 40 phoneme categories were generated at 100 Hz. Torchaudio pitch was detected over time with minimum and maximum pitch of 80 and 500, and the sparc pitch estimate uses a different algorithm with a range of 50 to 550 Hz. Waveform-derived features are stored in a fixed format holding static features extracted from the entire waveform and a temporal format that varies with the length of the recording.
Release 1.1 computed spectrograms with a 512-point FFT and no time-domain downsampling, and derived five types of data rather than the eight listed here; those parameters are historical to that release. The parameters stated above are those of releases 3.0.0 and 3.1.0.

    Cleaning Details
    The audit protocol generates missingness tables, checks distributions and outliers, checks categorical responses against the schema, and runs quality control metrics for audio tasks. The application includes models for volume standardization and noise cancellation to help ensure acoustic data quality.
