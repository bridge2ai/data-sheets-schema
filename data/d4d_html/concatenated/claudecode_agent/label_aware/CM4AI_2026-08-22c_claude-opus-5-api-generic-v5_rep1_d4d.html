Response
To deliver machine-readable hierarchical maps of cell architecture as AI-ready data, produced from multimodal interrogation of chromatin modifiers, metabolic enzymes and other proteins involved in cancer, neuropsychiatric and cardiac disorders in disease-relevant cell lines under perturbed and unperturbed conditions.
To map the spatiotemporal architecture of human cells and use these maps toward the grand challenge of interpretable genotype-phenotype learning.
To produce a matched resource of data and tools that enables the creation of "visible" machine learning systems built directly on knowledge maps of cell and tissue architecture, rather than difficult-to-interpret black-box models.
To produce ethical, AI-ready datasets of cell architecture that can be reused by the research community under minimally restrictive terms consistent with ethical guidelines, researcher attribution and research integrity.

Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence (CM4AI)
Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence - June 2026 Data Release (Beta)
Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence (CM4AI) is the Functional Genomics Data Generation Project of the U.S. National Institutes of Health Bridge to Artificial Intelligence (Bridge2AI) program. Its quarterly data releases provide AI-ready, multimodal measurements of human cell architecture, generated so that machine-readable hierarchical maps of cell structure can be built and applied to the challenge of interpretable genotype-phenotype learning. This record describes the June 2026 Data Release (Beta), version 2.0, published in the University of Virginia Dataverse under doi 10.18130/V3/HIGT4C. The release covers two disease-relevant human cell lines under perturbed and unperturbed conditions: the triple-negative breast cancer line MDA-MB-468, untreated and treated with paclitaxel or with vorinostat, and the KOLF2.1J induced pluripotent stem cell line, undifferentiated and differentiated into neural progenitor cells, neurons and cardiomyocytes. Four measurement modalities are represented: single-cell CRISPRi Perturb-seq for MDA-MB-468 cells with and without treatment and for undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs; size-exclusion chromatography-mass spectrometry (SEC-MS) protein-protein interaction data for MDA-MB-468 cells with and without treatment and for undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs and iPSC-derived neural progenitor cells, neurons and cardiomyocytes; affinity purification-mass spectrometry (AP-MS) data for treated MDA-MB-468 cells; and immunofluorescence confocal images of MDA-MB-468 cells with and without treatment. Ten ZIP archives totalling approximately 12.6 GB are distributed from the repository, while larger raw mass spectrometry and sequencing deposits are held in external repositories. Release outputs are packaged with provenance graphs and rich metadata as AI-ready datasets in RO-Crate format using the FAIRSCAPE framework, and the integration pipeline that turns these input streams into hierarchical cell maps is the Multi-Scale Integrated Cell (MuSIC) toolkit. Computed cell maps are not included in this release. The CM4AI project portal reports cumulative project totals of 1,374 protein interactions, 53,788 immunofluorescent images, 7,023 total proteins investigated, 11,739 genes targeted and 21.4 TB of data volume. CM4AI is a collaboration of the University of California San Diego, the University of California San Francisco, Stanford University, the University of Virginia, Yale University, the University of Texas at Austin, the University of Alabama at Birmingham, Simon Fraser University and the Hastings Center.

Response
In genomics and precision medicine, machine learning models are often black boxes that predict phenotypes from genotypes without conveying the mechanisms by which such translation occurs. CM4AI addresses this by supplying cell architecture maps on which interpretable models can be built.
Existing training resources for a biomedical workforce adept at both the data and life sciences are limited; CM4AI addresses this by broadly distributing its data and tools with accompanying training material.
Cell mapping technologies have typically been applied separately. CM4AI addresses the gap by generating matched protein-protein interaction, spatial localization and genetic perturbation data on the same cell lines and conditions so that the modalities can be integrated.

Mechanism Details
Protein-protein interaction data were acquired using two orthogonal mass spectrometry instruments and workflows: affinity purification-mass spectrometry on endogenously tagged cell lines, and size-exclusion chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry for proteome-wide complex and interaction mapping.
Spatial proteomics data were acquired using the Human Protein Atlas resource of antibodies, automated fixation and permeabilization protocols implemented on a pipetting robot for the MDA-MB-468 and KOLF2 cell lines, and confocal microscopy producing four-channel images.
Genetic perturbation data were acquired using a CRISPR lentiviral library targeting 100 chromatin factors with six guide RNAs per gene, MDA-MB-468 and KOLF2 CRISPR lines expressing inducible dCas9, and single-cell transcriptional readout on the 10x Genomics 3' HT kit.

Relationship Details
Instances are related through shared protein and gene identity. The same protein may appear as an AP-MS or SEC-MS interaction partner, be imaged by immunofluorescence and be targeted by a CRISPRi guide; the cell-mapping pipeline uses these correspondences to place proteins into a hierarchy of assemblies, represented as a directed acyclic graph in which an edge denotes containment of a smaller assembly by a larger one.
Instances are also related by cell line and condition. Matched measurements are made in MDA-MB-468 cells untreated and treated with paclitaxel or vorinostat, and in KOLF2.1J iPSCs undifferentiated and differentiated into neural progenitor cells, neurons and cardiomyocytes, so that architecture can be compared across treatment and differentiation states.
  • Missing Data Patterns
    The protein sets interrogated by the three main modalities incompletely overlap: the SEC-MS protein-protein interaction data, the immunofluorescence protein localization data and the CRISPRi Perturb-seq data do not cover the same proteins.
    Missing Data Causes
    The release is an interim Beta release. Some component datasets remain under temporary pre-publication embargo, computed cell maps are not yet included, and the CM4AI portal records that immunofluorescence images for the iPSC-derived cell types were still forthcoming while AP-MS interactomes for the breast cancer line were likewise being completed.
    Handling Strategy
    The project states that the dataset will be regularly updated and augmented through the end of the project in November 2026, on a quarterly release cadence, so that missing modalities and the computed cell maps are added in later releases.
Preprocessing Details
Downloaded protein-protein interaction networks are processed with the node2vec deep learning model to reduce dimensionality, producing a PPI embedding for each protein that captures information about that protein's interactions.
Immunofluorescence images are processed with a Human Protein Atlas deep learning model to reduce dimensionality, producing an image embedding for each protein that captures information about its subcellular localization.
The PPI and image embeddings are integrated into a single co-embedding per protein using contrastive deep learning. The integration model learns co-embeddings such that the original embeddings can be reconstructed with minimal information loss and proteins with similar PPI and image embeddings receive similar co-embeddings.
Community detection is performed on the all-by-all similarities of protein pairs in the co-embedding space, and the resulting hierarchy of protein communities is output as the final cell map.
Each segment of the pipeline calls the FAIRSCAPE client to validate its inputs and to build an RO-Crate output package containing the datasets, metadata and software or resolvable references to them, which is then dispatched to the FAIRSCAPE server for persistent identifier creation and computation of the provenance graph entailments.
Cleaning Details
Datasets and data elements are characterized and validated as part of AI-readiness packaging: complete schemas and validation procedures are supplied for all datasets in the provenance graph, data elements are described with JSON-Schema mini-data-dictionaries resolvable from the provenance graph metadata, and data elements are mapped to public ontology vocabularies where appropriate.
Single-cell CRISPR screen data from MDA-MB-468 cells under three conditions and from undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs were subjected to quality control before release; the CM4AI preprint records these data as being under quality control at the time of writing.
  • Labeling Details
    Cell maps are annotated in a two-pronged approach. First, cell maps are aligned to known protein function and pathway resources, including the Gene Ontology and Reactome, to determine which protein assemblies in the map have high overlap with known cell biology. Second, assemblies in the cell maps are annotated using a large language model approach developed by the project, which names sets of proteins and assigns a name confidence score to each proposed name.
  • Tools
    • node2vec
    • a Human Protein Atlas deep learning image model
    • the MuSIC contrastive co-embedding model
    • multi-resolution community detection
    • a large language model naming approach developed by the project
    Tool Descriptions
    node2vec produces a low-dimensional representation of each protein from its protein-protein interaction neighbourhood; the Human Protein Atlas deep learning model produces a low-dimensional representation of each protein from its four-channel immunofluorescence image; the contrastive deep learning model fuses these into a single co-embedding per protein; community detection over co-embedding similarities produces the hierarchy of protein assemblies; and the large language model names the resulting assemblies and assigns a confidence score to each name.

Uses

Has the dataset been used for any tasks already?

Response
Construct hierarchical maps of subcellular architecture, represented as directed acyclic graphs whose nodes are assemblies of proteins in proximity at a given scale, spanning large assemblies such as cell compartments down to small protein complexes and subunits.
Train and interrogate visible machine learning systems, including visible neural networks, that use cell maps to relate protein assemblies to cell-level phenotype predictions.
Analyze cell architectural changes and interactions in the presence of specific disease processes, treatment conditions or genetic perturbations.
Support interpretation of genetic variants and mutations in human genomics by mapping them onto protein assemblies.
Use Category
AI-ready datasets to support research in functional genomics
AI model training
Cellular process analysis
Cell architectural changes and interactions in presence of specific disease processes, treatment conditions, or genetic perturbations
Task Details
Integrative structure modeling of protein assemblies: the project reports a bioinformatics pipeline that annotates MuSIC communities with the available structural information about community members and their interactions, drawing on the Protein Data Bank, the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database, crosslinking mass spectrometry and prediction of disordered sequence segments, then ranks communities by the feasibility of integrative structure modeling.
Construction of new cell maps for other cell subtypes and disease states, using the released Cell Mapping Toolkit as a flexible and generalizable framework that lets researchers integrate additional input modalities.
Examples
The genome-scale CRISPRi perturbation data generated in KOLF2.1J induced pluripotent stem cells have been analyzed in "A Perturbation Cell Atlas of Human Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells" (Nourreddine S et al., bioRxiv 2024, doi 10.1101/2024.11.03.621734), which maps transcriptional and fitness phenotypes for 11,739 targeted genes and reports validation via phenotypic, protein-interaction and metabolic tracing assays.
CM4AI datasets, tools and standards were distributed with detailed tutorials at a CM4AI CodeFest held in March 2024, which provided training to 38 registrants and served as an end-to-end demonstration of the platform from data generation to AI-readiness packaging.
Discouragement Details
Analyses that depend on integrated, computed cell maps are not supported by this release. Predicted cell maps are not included and will be added in future releases; the current release is most suitable for bioinformatics analysis of the individual datasets.
Cross-modality integration should be undertaken with care. The SEC-MS protein-protein interaction data, the immunofluorescence protein localization data and the CRISPRi Perturb-seq data interrogate sets of proteins that incompletely overlap, so joint analyses will not cover the same proteins across modalities.
Prohibition Reason
These laboratory data are not to be used in clinical decision-making or in any context involving patient care without appropriate regulatory oversight and approval.
Commercial use is not permitted under the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license under which the data are distributed. The CM4AI preprint states that commercial use requires a separate license negotiation with the copyright holder, which is UCSD, Stanford and/or UCSF depending on the specific data package in question.
Impact Details
The project anticipates that its datasets and maps will underpin a new generation of interpretable, explainable biomedical AI, deepening understanding of processes of human disease and health, enabling development of novel cures, and assisting researchers and clinicians in equitably improving human lives.
Because the released data derive from two commercially available de-identified human cell lines, models trained on them may not generalize to the biological variation present in the wider population; the release records this as a potential source of bias for users.
The project states that its ethical obligations include anticipating potential applications of the data and derived AI/ML systems, integrating them into data governance frameworks to optimize benefits and mitigate potential risks, and promoting their use for the collective good.
