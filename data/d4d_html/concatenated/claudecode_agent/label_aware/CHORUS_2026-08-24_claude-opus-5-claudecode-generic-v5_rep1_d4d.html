CHoRUS dataset
Patient-Focused Collaborative Hospital Repository Uniting Standards (CHoRUS) for Equitable AI
CHoRUS is a multicenter, multimodal, high-resolution critical care dataset assembled by the Patient-Focused Collaborative Hospital Repository Uniting Standards for Equitable AI project, one of four data generation projects in the NIH-funded Bridge2AI consortium. Its stated overarching goal is to develop a publicly available, AI-ready critical care dataset while ensuring that the methods used promote privacy, accountability and clinical benefit. The data are collected retrospectively from patients with acute or critical illness at contributing hospitals and are held under controlled access. The project site describes a current released dataset of 50,000 patient admissions from intensive care, pediatric intensive care and neonatal intensive care units, comprising 1.6 billion rows of electronic health record data in the OMOP Common Data Model, 7,642 admissions with radiology data, and 23 Tb of waveform data. The anticipated final dataset is 100,000 patient admissions covering 9 data modalities from 14 data contributing hospitals. Nine data types are described: demographics, medication administration with dosing time-stamped at each infusion change or dose administration, procedures documented by providers, high-frequency nursing flowsheet documentation, diagnoses documented by providers, clinical notes extracted and tokenized using the OHNLP toolkit, imaging from PACS, waveform telemetry from bedside monitors and gateway or middleware systems, and EEG waveforms from hospital databases. Structured records are standardized to the OMOP Common Data Model; imaging uses DICOM, waveform telemetry uses WFDB, and EEG uses EDF+ and Persyst. Every data type is listed as controlled access. The project is organized around three pillars — Data (standards, tool development and optimization, and data acquisition), Ethics (ethical and trustworthy AI) and People (team science, and skill and workforce development). The collaboration spans 20 academic centers, of which 14 contribute as data acquisition centers, with more than 60 consortium members across 20 institutions. Beyond the assembled dataset, the project sequesters a holdout test set for external validation of models, develops unified standards to harmonize multimodal electronic health record, waveform, imaging and text data, and builds a visualization and annotation environment for labeling data with prediction targets.
