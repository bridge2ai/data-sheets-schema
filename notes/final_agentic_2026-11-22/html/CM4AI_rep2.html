Motivation

For what purpose was the dataset created?

Description
The project's overarching mission is to produce ethical, AI-ready datasets of cell architecture, inferred from multimodal data collected for human cell lines, to enable transformative biomedical AI research.
CM4AI states that it will deliver machine-readable hierarchical maps of cell architecture as AI-ready data, produced from multimodal interrogation of chromatin modifiers, metabolic enzymes and other proteins involved in cancer, neuropsychiatric and cardiac disorders, together with quarterly data releases of the map-input data streams.
The CM4AI data generation project seeks to map the spatiotemporal architecture of human cells and use these maps toward the grand challenge of interpretable genotype-phenotype learning.

Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence (CM4AI)
https://cm4ai.org/data-releases/
The CM4AI dataset is the series of quarterly data releases produced by Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence (CM4AI), the Functional Genomics Grand Challenge data generation project of the NIH Bridge2AI program. CM4AI is generating multi-modal data, including protein-protein interaction (PPI), spatial localization and genetic perturbation data, in MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells (with or without paclitaxel or vorinostat) and in induced pluripotent stem cells (with or without differentiation). The releases are deposited in the University of Virginia Dataverse; the current release (June 2026, doi:10.18130/V3/HIGT4C) includes Perturb-seq, SEC-MS, AP-MS and immunofluorescence (IF) image data. The Dataverse release records list the production locations as University of California San Diego, University of California San Francisco, Stanford University and University of Virginia, and the project website describes CM4AI as a collaboration of UCSD, UCSF, Stanford, UVA, Yale, UT Austin, UA Birmingham, Simon Fraser University and the Hastings Center. The project website reports a data volume of 21.4 TB. The May 2024 preprint describing the project states that its Data Module had completed parallel maps of protein-protein interactions, protein subcellular distributions and transcriptional states for a near-comprehensive set of chromatin regulators in breast cancer cells.

  • Description
    In genomics and precision medicine, machine learning models are often "black boxes" that predict phenotypes from genotypes without explaining the mechanisms by which that translation occurs. The NIH RePORTER project description states that the project will address this by creating a library of large-scale maps of cellular structure and function to stimulate research on "visible" machine learning systems.
Limitation Description
Some datasets are under temporary pre-publication embargo; the June 2026 release page lists the Perturb-seq data in undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs and in MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells (with or without treatment) as embargoed external data links.
The protein-protein interaction (SEC-MS), protein localization (IF imaging) and CRISPRi Perturb-seq data interrogate sets of proteins that incompletely overlap.
Computed (predicted) cell maps are not included in the current release; the release page states they will be added in future releases.
The June 2026 release is an interim release.
The current release is most suitable for bioinformatics analysis of the individual datasets.
Meaningful analysis requires domain expertise.
Bias Description
Data in the release were derived from commercially available de-identified human cell lines.
The data do not represent all biological variants that may be seen in the population at large.

Immunofluorescence (IF) subcellular microscopy generates the spatial proteomics data stream. The May 2024 preprint reports completed spatial proteomics mapping of 100 chromatin regulators in MDA-MB-468 cells (with or without paclitaxel or vorinostat). Release file descriptions state that the images were acquired by immunofluorescence-based staining (ICC-IF) and confocal microscopy in the Lundberg Lab at Stanford University.
Immunofluorescence subcellular microscopy
AP-MS generates protein-protein interaction data. As of the May 2024 preprint, 17 genes had been endogenously tagged in MDA-MB-468 cells and AP-MS data acquired under three conditions (untreated, paclitaxel and vorinostat treated). The June 2026 release includes AP-MS data for treated MDA-MB-468 cells.
Affinity purification mass spectrometry (AP-MS)
SEC-MS generates proteome-wide protein complex and interaction data. The releases include SEC-MS data for MDA-MB-468 cells with or without treatment, undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs, and iPSC-derived NPCs, neurons and cardiomyocytes.
Size exclusion chromatography mass spectrometry (SEC-MS)
Single-cell CRISPR-Cas perturbation screens by cell type. The May 2024 preprint reports single-cell CRISPR screens of 100 chromatin regulators (with or without drug) in MDA-MB-468 cells and in undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs using the 10x Genomics 3'HT kit, with the resulting data then being quality-controlled.
Single-cell CRISPR perturbation screens (Perturb-seq)

DescriptionNameSource Caveats
Input data streams are integrated via the Multi-Scale Integrated Cell (MuSIC) software pipeline, which integrates localization and interaction data using self-supervised embedding approaches from deep learning, followed by algorithmic community detection at multiple resolutions to produce a hierarchy of protein assemblies (a cell map).
MuSIC data integration pipeline
Described in the May 2024 preprint; it applies to the cell maps, which the June 2026 release states are not included in that release.
Downloaded PPI networks are processed with the node2vec deep learning model to produce a PPI embedding for each protein, and IF images are processed with a Human Protein Atlas deep learning model to produce an image embedding that carries protein localization information.
Embedding generation
The PPI and image embeddings are integrated with contrastive deep learning into a co-embedding for each protein.
Co-embedding
Community detection is performed on the all-by-all similarities of protein pairs in the co-embedding space, and the resulting hierarchy of protein communities is output as the cell map.
Community detection and hierarchy creation
  • Description
    Cell maps are annotated in two ways: assemblies are aligned to known protein function and pathway resources, including the Gene Ontology and Reactome, to find assemblies with high overlap with known cell biology; and assemblies are named with a large language model approach that assigns a name and a name confidence score to each set of proteins.
    Source Caveats
    Described in the May 2024 preprint as the hierarchy-evaluation step of the MuSIC pipeline; computed cell maps are not included in the June 2026 release.
Deidentification Details
The release pages state "De-identified Samples: Yes". The May 2024 preprint states that CM4AI data are considered to be de-identified because they cannot be matched, with current knowledge, to a human subject.
