Enable AI/ML research on type 2 diabetes and salutogenesis
The purpose for creating the dataset was to enable future generations of artificial intelligence/machine learning (AI/ML) research to provide critical insights into type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), including salutogenic pathways to return to health. The primary goal of AI-READI is to collect and publicly share a multimodal, AI-ready dataset for studying the pathogenesis and salutogenesis (the pathway from disease to health) of T2DM; AI-READI is a data collection project on T2DM intended to facilitate the use of AI/ML approaches to study salutogenesis.
Foundational dataset for a temporal atlas of T2DM
The long-term objective stated for the project is to develop a foundational dataset in diabetes, agnostic to existing classification criteria, which can be used to reconstruct a temporal atlas of T2DM development and reversal towards health (salutogenesis).

Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Version 3.0.0 of the AI-READI dataset, published on the FAIRhub platform. The AI-READI project seeks to create and share a flagship ethically sourced dataset of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). The dataset consists of data collected from individuals with and without T2DM and harmonized across 3 data collection sites in the USA. The FAIRhub metadata state that this version contains data from 2280 participants collected between July 19, 2023 and May 1, 2025. Data are collected in a multivariable protocol containing over 10 domains, and the dataset includes survey data, clinical data, retinal imaging data, environmental sensor data, continuous glucose monitor data, wearable activity monitor data and electrocardiogram data; these encompass tabular data, imaging data and physiological signal/waveform data, and there is no unstructured text data. The retinal imaging protocol captures imaging from multiple devices. The dataset is described as hypothesis-agnostic, aimed at facilitating multiple potential downstream AI/ML applications. Data are organized following the Clinical Dataset Structure (CDS) v0.1.1: at the root level there is one directory per data type, within each data type one folder per modality, within each modality one folder per device and within each device one folder per participant. All data files are in formats accessible with free software commonly used for such data types, so no specific software is required. The v3.0.0 documentation states that the public dataset will include survey data, blood and urine lab results, fitness activity levels, clinical measurements, retinal images, ECG, blood sugar levels and environmental variables such as home air quality. Data were collected in the USA. The FAIRhub landing page states that a smaller version is available for pipeline development.

NameResponse
Limitations of existing large datasets for AI/ML training
To address inherent limitations of many existing large data sets for AI/ML training, the cohort is designed to be balanced in race/ethnicity, T2DM severity and biological sex.
Lack of racial and ethnic diversity in T2DM study samples
The BMJ Open protocol paper cites a meta-epidemiological review of T2DM studies published from 2000 to 2020 indicating that samples lacked racial and ethnic diversity.
Lack of AI-ready data for training AI models
A key issue for AI-based approaches to T2DM is the lack of data ready for training AI models (described in the Nature Metabolism comment as a lack of diverse datasets that are AI ready); the AI-READI dataset is intended to fill this gap.
Lack of large, high-quality multimodal datasets
The ability to understand and affect the course of complex, multi-organ diseases such as T2DM has been limited by a lack of well-designed, high quality, large and inclusive multimodal datasets.
Analyses not feasible with claims or EHR data
The data acquisition protocol across multiple domains was designed to enable downstream AI/ML analyses that may not be feasible with existing data sources such as claims or electronic health records data.

Random SamplingNameStrategies
FalseTriple-balanced recruitment design
Rather than targeting the demographic distribution of the US population, the study is triple-balanced by recruiting in equal proportions four race/ethnic groups (Asian, Black, White, Hispanic), four categories of T2DM (no diabetes, pre-diabetes/lifestyle-controlled diabetes, diabetes treated with oral medications or non-insulin-injectable medications, and insulin-controlled diabetes) and biological males and females; recruitment sampling procedures aimed at approximately equal distribution across sex, race and diabetes severity. The study registration states an aim to recruit approximately 1000 patients into each of the four groups, with subjects age- and sex-matched within and between groups, and describes the sample as a non-probability sample. Participants are recruited at three sites (University of Alabama at Birmingham, University of California, San Diego, and University of Washington), chosen for geographic diversity and diverse representation across racial and ethnic groups; uniform data collection protocols were implemented for all subjects across sites. Inclusion criteria: able to provide consent, 40 years or older, persons with or without type 2 diabetes, and able to speak and read English. Exclusion criteria: pregnancy, gestational diabetes, type 1 diabetes and (per the study registration and IRB protocol) age older than 85 years.
EHR-based recruitment in waves
Patients with T2DM and pre-diabetes are identified by screening electronic health records for diagnosis codes at each site, and participants are recruited in waves whose composition is monitored and adjusted by under- and oversampling groups as needed. The IRB protocol describes UAB selecting a stratified sample of potential participants based on the sampling scheme, with approximately 50,000 invitation letters per site planned to be sent by mail and email over the recruitment period.
Site-specific recruitment priorities
The IRB protocol states that, based on local demographics, UW will concentrate on recruiting Asian>Hispanic>Black participants, UC San Diego will prioritize Hispanic>Asian>Black participants and UAB will prioritize Black>Hispanic>Asian participants, with all sites recruiting white participants; all race and ethnicity information is self-reported at screening and enrollment.

Mapping to data standards
Once data were sent from each site to the central project team, all data were mapped to standardized terminologies when possible, such as the OMOP Common Data Model for clinical data and DICOM for medical imaging; some images required conversion to a DICOM standard format before upload.

For some devices, data are transformed from their proprietary state into a standard model format before upload to FAIRhub; for example, the protocol paper's device table lists imaging exports in .fda and .sdt file formats converted to DICOM for the data set, and, among the at-home monitoring devices, an export in .FIT file format converted to the mHealth standard.
The protocol paper's Table 4 text extraction does not make explicit which imaging device or monitoring device each export format belongs to, so formats are not attributed to individual devices here.

The Nature Metabolism comment (November 2024) states that the public set is stripped of Protected Health Information as defined by the HIPAA Privacy Rule via the Safe Harbor method, together with information related to sex and race/ethnicity.
