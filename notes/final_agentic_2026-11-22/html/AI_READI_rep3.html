Motivation

For what purpose was the dataset created?

DescriptionName
The dataset was created to enable future generations of artificial intelligence/machine learning (AI/ML) research to provide critical insights into type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), including salutogenic pathways to return to health. The primary goal of AI-READI is to collect and publicly share a multimodal, AI-ready dataset for studying the pathogenesis and salutogenesis (the pathway from disease to health) of T2DM; the documentation states the goal as better understanding salutogenesis in T2DM.
Enable AI/ML research into T2DM salutogenesis
The NIH RePORTER abstract states the project's long-term objective as developing a foundational dataset in diabetes, agnostic to existing classification criteria, which can be used to reconstruct a temporal atlas of T2DM development and reversal towards health.
Foundational dataset for a temporal atlas of T2DM

doi:10.60775/fairhub.3
AI-READI dataset
Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Version 3.0.0 of the AI-READI dataset, published on FAIRhub. It consists of data collected from individuals with and without type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), harmonized across three data collection sites. FAIRhub states that it contains data from 2280 participants collected between July 19, 2023 and May 1, 2025, across multiple modalities: survey data, clinical data, retinal imaging data, environmental sensor data, continuous glucose monitor data and wearable activity monitor data. These comprise tabular data, imaging data and physiological signal/waveform data; the healthsheet states that no unstructured text data are included. The dataset contains 356,343 files and is around 3.82 TB in size. It is organized following the Clinical Dataset Structure (CDS) v0.1.1: one directory per data type at the root level, then one folder per modality, one per device used to collect that modality, and one per participant. The AI-READI project seeks to create and share a flagship ethically sourced dataset of type 2 diabetes.

Description
A meta-epidemiological review of T2DM studies published from 2000 to 2020 found that samples lacked racial and ethnic diversity; the BMJ Open protocol presents AI-READI's balanced cohort as a step toward addressing these demographic inequities in research.
A major barrier to AI-based approaches in T2DM has been the lack of diverse datasets that are ready for training AI models; the AI-READI dataset is intended to fill this gap.
The ability to understand and affect the course of complex, multi-organ diseases such as T2DM has been limited by a lack of well-designed, high quality and large multimodal datasets.
The data acquisition protocol spans multiple domains to enable downstream AI/ML analyses that may not be feasible with existing data sources such as claims or electronic health records data.

  1. Name
    Triple-balanced non-probability recruitment
    Description
    Recruitment sampling procedures aimed at approximately equal distribution of participants across sex, race and diabetes severity. Rather than targeting the demographic distribution of the US population, the study recruits in equal proportions four race/ethnic groups (Asian, Black, Hispanic, White), four categories of T2DM and biological males and females.
    Sample
    True
    Random Sampling
    False
    Source Data
    The study base is all patients aged 40 or older who had a medical encounter within each health system site (UAB, UCSD, UW) between 2020 and 2025; recruitment pools are identified from electronic health records using ICD-10 codes and demographic data.
    Strategies
    Inclusion: able to provide consent; 40 years or older; persons with or without type 2 diabetes; must speak and read English. Exclusion: pregnancy; gestational diabetes; type 1 diabetes; adults older than 85 years (the last is listed in the FAIRhub eligibility module and IRB protocol). Recruitment selects individuals by clinical diagnosis (ICD-10 codes) and demographic data from the electronic health records of the study sites.
    Notes
    The FAIRhub study description records the sampling method as a non-probability sample. Data were collected in the USA at three sites: the University of Alabama at Birmingham, the University of California San Diego and the University of Washington, chosen for geographic diversity and diverse racial and ethnic representation. The IRB protocol describes UAB selecting a stratified sample of potential participants from the combined site lists, and site priorities (UW: Asian>Hispanic>Black; UCSD: Hispanic>Asian>Black; UAB: Black>Hispanic>Asian; all sites recruit White participants). Enrollment targets are stated as plans: 4,000 participants over 2022-2026 (Nature Metabolism) and approximately 1,000 per diabetes group.
