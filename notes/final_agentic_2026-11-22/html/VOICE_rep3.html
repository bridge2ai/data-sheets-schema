Motivation

For what purpose was the dataset created?

ResponseSource Caveats
The Bridge2AI-Voice consortium aims to generate a large-scale, ethically sourced voice, speech, and cough database linked to health metadata in order to support AI-driven research.
Bridge2AI-Voice seeks to create a flagship, standardized, and ethically sourced dataset of 10,000 voices linked to health information to fuel research and discovery in voice biomarkers (a stated target, not a count of the current release).
Target sizes differ across sources. The project documentation (manifest tier 2) states a flagship dataset of 10,000 voices; the IRB protocol (also tier 2) states a sample size of 30 000 participants; the JAMA Otolaryngology viewpoint (tier 3) states a primary deliverable of a publicly available database of 30 000 human voices. The two tier-2 sources share a rank and are targets stated at different times, so both are recorded rather than one selected. Version 3.1 contains data for 833 participants.
Specific Aim 1 of the NIH project: to build a multi-modal, multi-institutional, large scale, diverse and ethically sourced human voice database linked to other biomarkers of health that is AI/ML friendly to fuel voice AI research.
To facilitate the use of voice as a non-invasive, cost-effective biomarker for the screening, diagnosis, and monitoring of a wide range of health conditions.

Bridge2AI-Voice
Bridge2AI-Voice: An ethically-sourced, diverse voice dataset linked to health information
A large, ethically sourced, and demographically diverse voice dataset linked to health information, released by the NIH Bridge2AI initiative. Version 3.1.0 contains data for 833 participants across five sites in North America; the healthsheet names the countries of data collection as the USA and Canada and states that five recording sites are included. The release contains data considered low risk, including derived audio features such as spectrograms but not the original voice recordings, together with detailed demographic, clinical, and validated questionnaire data and metadata related to the specific audio task and recording. The underlying samples come from conventional acoustic tasks including respiratory sounds, cough sounds, and free speech prompts.

Response
Existing literature has studied voice only in small- to medium-sized data sets with limited data outside of acoustic measures, not linked to multi-modal health data.
Comparing studies and pooling data is challenging due to a lack of existing standards in how voice and other acoustics are measured and collected.
The dataset was created to address a gap in the availability of large-scale, diverse, and well-documented voice data for use in clinical machine-learning applications; previous studies' sample sizes were too small, or they lacked the key metadata needed for training robust, clinically useful models.

A novel smartphone application created to collect standardized recordings of acoustic tasks, validated patient questionnaires, and validated patient reported outcomes; data collection is performed via the app, which is linked to electronic health records (EHR).
The app collects breathing sounds and voice, speech, and linguistic tasks, along with health information through surveys and validated questionnaires. A total of 22 acoustic tasks are recorded through the app. Detailed demographic data and surveys about confounding factors such as smoking and drinking history are collected through the app. The healthsheet states the data were collected using an iPad app; the PhysioNet release states data collection was conducted using a custom application on a tablet with a headset used when possible.
Bridge2AI-Voice App
Data is collected on iPads (9th or 10th generation) and iPad Air (5th generation) using an Avid AE-36 microphone and an Apple dongle to connect it to the iPad.

Certain aspects of the data (e.g., transcription) were generated using off-the-shelf models that have not been audited for correctness.

Method
HIPAA Safe Harbor identifiers were removed; the project documentation states the dataset applies the HIPAA de-identification rules, and that it was de-identified through removal of all audio data and certain sensitive fields identified by a team of ethicists.
Identifiers Removed
All direct identifiers, including name, civic address, and social security numbers; state and province (country of data collection was retained).
Deidentification Details
Identifiable information (under HIPAA and the Common Rule), as well as data considered sensitive, have been removed. Indirect identifiers were removed where these created a significant risk of participant re-identification. All raw voice data was removed, as this data has the potential to cause individual re-identification or to be used for illicit or unauthorized purposes. Raw audio transcripts were reviewed, and any audio recordings that contained potentially identifying information and external voices were removed from the release. Spectrograms and similar features were excluded if the audio contained free speech; static and other features which do not encode the sensitive information were retained. Spectrograms, Mel spectrograms, MFC coefficients, PPGs, and EMAs for sensitive records and audio checks have been removed from v3.1. The documentation also states that spectrograms, MFCCs, Mel spectrograms, transcriptions, EMAs, and PPGs from open-response prompts are removed from the feature-only dataset.

The feature-only dataset provides AI-ready derivations from the raw audio (project documentation).
Features extracted include OpenSmile eGeMaps features per audio file; Parselmouth/Praat speech features for any speech tasks; Torchaudio-based pitch contour, spectrograms, mel spectrogram, and MFCCs; Speech Articulatory Coding (sparc)-based features including electromagnetic articulography (EMA) estimates, plus loudness, periodicity, and pitch measures; and phonetic posteriorgrams (PPGs). Waveform-derived features are stored in a fixed feature format of static features extracted from the entire waveform and a temporal format that varies for each audio file depending on the length of recording.
Raw audio was preprocessed by converting to monaural and resampling to 16 kHz with a Butterworth anti-aliasing filter applied (PhysioNet release 3.1.0).
Spectrograms were computed using the short-time Fast Fourier Transform with a 25ms window size, 10ms hop length, and a 400-point FFT, and further downsampled by a factor of two in the time domain. 60 MFCCs were extracted from the spectrograms. Mel spectrograms were computed with the same parameters and 60 Mels. Articulatory features were generated with the Speech Articulatory Coding (sparc) package at 50Hz. Acoustic features were extracted using OpenSMILE. Phonetic and prosodic features were computed using Parselmouth and Praat. Phonetic posteriorgrams at 100Hz were generated via the ppgs package.

Version Details
Release notes: v1.1 added Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs); v2.0 provided data for an additional 136 new participants; v2.0.1 made corrections in the authorship list; v3.0.0 was a major update with new data for an additional 391 participants; v3.1.0 was a minor update with no new participants but some additional data released for certain participants, adding files related to audio quality metrics and per-audio metadata, updated validated diagnosis information with back-filled information, and some phenotypic files rearranged and gold standard variables renamed. An earlier version of the feature-only dataset is available on Health Data Nexus, which provides cloud compute alongside the dataset rather than allowing data downloads. The healthsheet, which describes distribution through the Health Data Nexus platform, states the data was published and made available at the end of November 2024. By default, older versions continue to be supported, hosted, and made available, each version has a unique DOI, and the publishers reserve the right to remove access to older versions. A changelog for each dataset version is published online with the dataset metadata.
