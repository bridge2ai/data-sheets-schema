https://cm4ai.org/
CM4AI
Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence
CM4AI (Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence) is the Functional Genomics Grand Challenge in the NIH Bridge2AI program. CM4AI is generating multi-modal data including protein-protein interaction (PPI), spatial localization, and genetic perturbation data in MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells (+/- paclitaxel or vorinostat) and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) (+/- differentiation), and releases data quarterly; the captured releases are deposited in the University of Virginia Dataverse, each under its own DOI. The current release, the June 2026 Data Release (Beta), includes Perturb-seq data for MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells +/- treatment and undifferentiated (parental) KOLF2.1J iPSCs; SEC-MS data for MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells +/- treatment, undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs, and iPSC-derived neuron progenitor cells (NPCs), neurons, and cardiomyocytes; AP-MS data for MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells + treatment; and IF images in MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells +/- treatment. The release states that these data are not yet in completed final form and that computed cell maps are not included. The project's data releases page states that CM4AI will deliver machine-readable hierarchical maps of cell architecture as AI-ready data, together with quarterly data releases of map-input data streams, and that CM4AI is a collaboration of UCSD, UCSF, Stanford, UVA, Yale, UT Austin, UA Birmingham, Simon Fraser University, and the Hastings Center. The project website's "Data Insights" panel reports 1,374 protein interactions, 53,788 immunofluorescent images, 7,023 total proteins investigated, 11,739 genes targeted and a data volume of 21.4 TB.

    AI-readiness packaging in RO-Crate format with FAIRSCAPE
    Preprocessing Details
    CM4AI output data are packaged with provenance graphs and rich metadata as AI-ready datasets in RO-Crate format using the FAIRSCAPE framework. The 2024 project preprint states that the project's FAIR AI-Readiness metadata and validation toolkits have been used to package and release its cell-line perturbation and measurement data. As that preprint describes the framework, the FAIRSCAPE client package (FAIRSCAPE-CLI) creates an RO-Crate package with the datasets, metadata, and software, or resolvable references to these components; the RO-Crates are then sent to the FAIRSCAPE server, where they are registered and assigned persistent identifiers.

The June 2026 Data Release states "De-identified Samples: Yes" and that the data in the release were derived from commercially available de-identified human cell lines.
