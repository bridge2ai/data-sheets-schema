Response
The Voice project seeks to create an ethically sourced flagship dataset to enable future research in artificial intelligence and support critical insights into the use of voice as a biomarker of health.
The project documentation states that the dataset aims to provide an ethically sourced, large, and diverse dataset to develop, benchmark, or validate clinically applicable AI/ML models, with the goal of facilitating the use of voice as a non-invasive, cost-effective biomarker for the screening, diagnosis, and monitoring of a wide range of health conditions.
The study protocol states its primary objective as building a large multi-institutional database of human voices, speech and respiratory sounds that is ethically sourced, diverse, and linked to multimodal health biomarkers to fuel voice AI research.

Bridge2AI-Voice: An ethically-sourced, diverse voice dataset linked to health information
Bridge2AI-Voice is a collection of data derived from voice recordings with corresponding clinical information. Version 3.1 contains data for 833 participants across five sites in North America. Participants were selected based on known conditions which manifest within the voice waveform, including voice disorders, neurological disorders, mood disorders, and respiratory disorders. The release contains data considered low risk, including derivations such as spectrograms but not the original voice recordings; detailed demographic, clinical, and validated questionnaire data are also made available. The dataset contains derived audio data features, phenotypic information acquired during data collection, and metadata related to the specific audio task and recording.

ResponseSource Caveats
The dataset was created to address a gap in the availability of large-scale, diverse, and well-documented voice data for use in clinical machine-learning applications. Previous studies on machine learning-based voice diagnosis produced promising results, but their sample sizes were too small, or they lacked the key metadata needed for training robust, clinically useful models.
Comparing studies and pooling data is challenging due to a lack of existing standards in how voice and other acoustics are measured and collected.
Within the industry, datasets are often private, limiting the ability to audit machine-learning models and the accuracy of training data labels.
Stated in the audiomics viewpoint as a limitation of the field that the project team aims to address.

Version Details
Version 3.1.0 (DOI 10.13026/8xbn-nq66) is a minor update with no new participants, but some additional data released for certain participants; it adds files related to audio quality metrics and per-audio metadata, updates validated diagnosis information with back-filled information, and rearranges some phenotype files. Version 3.0.0 (DOI 10.13026/k81f-qr68) was a major update with new data for an additional 391 participants; the project documentation describes version 3.0 as containing ~61,937 voice-derived recordings from 833 adult participants. Version 2.0 provided data for an additional 136 new participants. Version 1.1 (DOI 10.13026/249v-w155) added Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients, and its files are no longer available. Version 1.0, the initial release, provided 12,523 recordings for 306 participants and was published on the Health Data Nexus (DOI 10.57764/qb6h-em84); the project documentation states that this earlier feature-only version provides cloud compute alongside the dataset rather than allowing data downloads. The healthsheet states that by default older versions will continue to be supported, hosted, and made available, that each version has a unique DOI, and that the publishers reserve the right to remove access to older versions.
