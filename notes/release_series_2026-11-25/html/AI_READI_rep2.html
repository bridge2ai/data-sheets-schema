Response
The dataset was created to enable future generations of artificial intelligence/machine learning (AI/ML) research to provide critical insights into type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), including salutogenic pathways to return to health. The goal is to better understand salutogenesis (the pathway from disease to health) in T2DM.
An additional aim of the AI-READI project is to share its blueprint for collecting and sharing AI-ready datasets so that future data generation projects can follow it.

AI-READI dataset
Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Version 3.0.0 of the AI-READI dataset, a dataset of data collected from individuals with and without type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and harmonized across 3 data collection sites. This version contains data from 2280 participants collected between July 19, 2023 and May 1, 2025. Data from multiple modalities are included: survey data, clinical data, retinal imaging data, environmental sensor data, continuous glucose monitor data and wearable activity monitor data, encompassing tabular data, imaging data and physiological signal/waveform data, with no unstructured text data. The data contain no protected health information (PHI); information related to the sex and race/ethnicity of the participants and to medication used has been removed. The dataset contains 356,343 files and is around 3.82 TB in size.

Response
AI-based approaches may help improve the understanding of T2DM, but a key issue is the lack of data ready for training AI models; the AI-READI dataset is intended to fill this gap.
To address inherent limitations of many existing large datasets for AI/ML training, the cohort is designed to be balanced in race/ethnicity, T2DM severity and biological sex.
The ability to understand and affect the course of complex, multi-organ diseases such as T2DM has been limited by a lack of well-designed, high quality and large multimodal datasets.

    Why Not Representative
    Rather than targeting the demographic distribution of the US population, the study is triple-balanced by recruiting race/ethnic groups, categories of T2DM and biological males and females in equal proportions, because building balanced datasets is critical for the development of unbiased machine learning models.
    Source Data
    The study base is all patients aged 40 years or older who had a medical encounter within each health system site. Recruitment pools were identified by screening electronic health records for diabetes and prediabetes ICD-10 codes.
    Strategies
    Non-probability sample. Recruitment sampling procedures aimed at approximately equal distribution of participants across diabetes severity; participants are recruited in waves, and the IRB protocol describes a stratified sample of potential participants selected from the combined site lists according to the sampling scheme. Inclusion criteria: able to provide consent, 40 years old or older, persons with or without type 2 diabetes, must speak and read English. Exclusion criteria: pregnancy, gestational diabetes, Type 1 diabetes and, in the study description, adults older than 85 years of age.
    Source Caveats
    The healthsheet states that the dataset contains all possible instances (all enrolled participants), which is what is_sample records; the study description gives the sampling method as Non-Probability Sample, which is what is_random records.

Preprocessing DetailsSource Caveats
All data were mapped to standardized terminologies when possible, such as the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP) Common Data Model for clinical data and the Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) format for retinal imaging.
Images are exported from imaging devices in their raw format, and some images required conversion to a DICOM standard format before upload; the BMJ Open protocol paper's device table lists exports in .fda and .sdt file formats converted to DICOM for the dataset.
The BMJ Open protocol paper's monitoring device table lists an export in .FIT file format converted to the mHealth standard for the dataset; in the dataset structure description, all data files of the wearable activity monitor and wearable blood glucose directories follow the Open mHealth standard.
The device table is flattened in the extracted text, so which monitoring device the .FIT cell belongs to cannot be read from it; no device is attributed here.
Cleaning Details
The Data Manager at each data site is responsible for the quality control of all data before uploading to FAIRhub.
Quality control at the time of data entry/acquisition: clinical data outside of expected min/max ranges were flagged in REDCap; Data Managers and clinical research coordinators examined participant records for credibility, incorrect flow through skip patterns, missing data that could be filled from other portions of the record, and omitted or duplicated records. Editing was done only under the guidance and approval of the site PI.
The dataset has not undergone any formal external audits, but has been reviewed internally by AI-READI team members for quality checks and to ensure that no personally identifiable information was accidentally included.
