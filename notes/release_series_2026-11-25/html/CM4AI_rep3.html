CM4AI
Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence (CM4AI)
CM4AI is the dataset produced by the Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence (CM4AI) data generation project, the Functional Genomics Grand Challenge in the NIH Bridge2AI program. The project states that it will deliver machine-readable hierarchical maps of cell architecture as AI-ready data, together with quarterly data releases of map-input data streams. The data are multimodal: protein-protein interaction (PPI), spatial localization, and genetic perturbation data in MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells (with and without paclitaxel or vorinostat) and in induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs, with and without differentiation). The current release, the June 2026 Data Release (Beta), includes perturb-seq data for MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells with and without treatment and for undifferentiated (parental) KOLF2.1J iPSCs; SEC-MS data for MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells with and without treatment, undifferentiated KOLF2.1J iPSCs, and iPSC-derived neuron progenitor cells (NPCs), neurons, and cardiomyocytes; AP-MS data for treated MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells; and immunofluorescence (IF) images of MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells with and without treatment. Computed cell maps are not included in the current release. The releases are deposited in the University of Virginia Dataverse and are recorded under resources. The project website reports, as project-wide figures, 7,023 total proteins investigated and a data volume of 21.4 TB. CM4AI is a collaboration of UCSD, UCSF, Stanford, UVA, Yale, UT Austin, UA Birmingham, Simon Fraser University, and the Hastings Center. The Dataverse record of the current release names Ideker Trey (University of California San Diego) as its Point of Contact and directs contact through the repository's email button.

The October 2025 release describes its SEC-MS files as generated by size exclusion chromatography-mass spectroscopy on undifferentiated KOLF2.1J human induced pluripotent stem cells, and following the treatment of vorinostat or paclitaxel on MDA-MB468 human breast cancer cells, in the Nevan Krogan laboratory at the University of California San Francisco.
Size exclusion chromatography-mass spectroscopy (SEC-MS)
The October 2025 release describes its image files as showing the spatial localization of proteins of interest in MDA-MB-468 cells as imaged by immunofluorescence-based staining (ICC-IF) and confocal microscopy in the Lundberg Lab at Stanford University. Nuclei were stained with DAPI (blue channel); endoplasmic reticulum with a calreticulin antibody (yellow channel); microtubules with tubulin antibody (red channel); and antibody against protein of interest (green channel).

The October 2025 release describes its perturb-seq files as an expressed genome-scale CRISPRi Perturbation Cell Atlas in KOLF2.1J human induced pluripotent stem cells mapping transcriptional and fitness phenotypes associated with 11,739 targeted genes, with raw sequence data in a separate file.
CRISPRi perturb-seq

Limitation Description
The current release is an interim release; the data are not yet in completed final form.
The current release does not contain predicted cell maps; the release states that these will be added in future releases.
The current release is most suitable for bioinformatics analysis of the individual datasets.
Requires domain expertise for meaningful analysis.
The protein-protein interaction (SEC-MS), protein localization (IF imaging), and CRISPRi perturb-seq data interrogate sets of proteins which incompletely overlap.
Some datasets are under temporary pre-publication embargo.
Bias Description
Data in the current release was derived from commercially available de-identified human cell lines.
Does not represent all biological variants which may be seen in the population at large.

Deidentification Details
The current release states De-identified Samples: Yes and that the data were derived from commercially available de-identified human cell lines. The May 2024 preprint states that CM4AI data are non-clinical (from tissue cultures) and are considered to be de-identified as they cannot be matched, with current knowledge, to a human subject.
