ResponseSource Caveats
The availability of large-scale pediatric databases is limited. Data collection in pediatric populations poses unique challenges such as privacy issues, consent processes and the need for developmentally appropriate tasks, and these factors have contributed to the lack of publicly available pediatric data sets that enable machine learning for pediatric voice analysis.
Pediatric and adult voice and speech production are fundamentally different because the respiratory system and larynx undergo rapid functional maturation throughout childhood, so normative data from adults cannot be generalized to pediatric populations.
The literature is sparser in terms of pediatric voice and speech analysis, partly due to ethical concerns and challenges in data acquisition for this cohort; because voice and speech vary with a child's development, a voice database of a normal cohort of different age groups is described as key to helping machine learning models diagnose age-specific speech delays and disorders.
From the background section of the project's IRB protocol (Bridge2AI Voice Data Acquisition) on its pediatric disease category, written as a statement of intent before data collection.
  • Source Data
    Patients and healthy volunteers at the Hospital for Sick Children; non-patients were recruited through research postings. Pediatric participants are recruited strictly from the Hospital for Sick Children (SickKids).
    Strategies
    Eligibility-based enrollment: inclusion criteria were 2 to 18 years of age and English proficiency; exclusion criteria were being over 18 years of age and being non-verbal. Non-patients were evaluated for eligibility against the same inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Mechanism DetailsUsed Software
All data was collected through customized software, reproschema-ui, on tablets, with the Bridge2AI-Voice pediatric protocol. All participants completed the recording and demographic data collection in one session.
{'id': 'doi:10.13026/mf9s-5r03#software-reproschema-ui', 'name': 'reproschema-ui'}
A headset was used to record for most participants.
The remaining recordings utilized the built-in tablet microphone, due to low tolerance of wearing headphones or existing complex medical conditions.
Acquisition DetailsSource Caveats
A standardized age-appropriate protocol collected demographic information, voice and speech related questionnaires, and questionnaires inquiring about medical history. For participants without adequate comprehension and familiarity with their past medical history, parents or decision-makers completed the survey during the recording on a separate tablet.
Acoustic tasks listed for the pediatric (Peds) part of the Bridge2AI-Voice protocol: ABC's (recalling alphabet), Ready For School (recalling a typical day preparing for school), Favorite Show (recalling favorite shows), Favorite Food (describing their favorite food), Outside of School (after school activities description), Months (listing the months), Counting, Naming Animals (listing animals), Naming Food (listing foods), Identifying Pictures (picture identification), Picture Description (Pediatrics), Long Sounds (sustained /ee/ and /ah/ sounds), Noisy Sounds, Caterpillar Passage (Pediatrics) (validated passage), Repeat Words (word repetition), Role naming (recalling days, months, and counting from 60 to 70), Repeat Sentences (sentence recall) and Silly Sounds (/PUH/ /TUH/ /KUH/ /PUH TUH KUH/).
From the project documentation's table of acoustic tasks in the protocol; the table states what the protocol contains, not which tasks have recordings in release 1.1.0.
