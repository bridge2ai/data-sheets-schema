Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
The AI-READI flagship dataset is a multimodal, AI-ready dataset of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) collected from individuals with and without T2DM and harmonized across three data collection sites (University of Alabama at Birmingham, University of California San Diego, and University of Washington). Version 3.0.0 contains data from 2280 participants collected between July 19, 2023 and May 1, 2025; it comprises 356,343 files and is around 3.82 TB in size. Modalities include survey and clinical data mapped to the OMOP Common Data Model, blood and urine laboratory results, physical measurements and vision testing, cognitive (MoCA) and monofilament testing, 12-lead electrocardiography, retinal photography, optical coherence tomography (OCT), OCT angiography (OCTA), fluorescence lifetime imaging ophthalmoscopy (FLIO), continuous glucose monitoring, wearable activity monitoring, and home environmental sensor data; these encompass tabular data, imaging data and physiological signal/waveform data, and no unstructured text. Recruitment sampling aimed at an approximately equal distribution of participants across diabetes severity, sex and race/ethnicity so that the collection would support downstream AI/ML analyses of salutogenesis, the pathway from disease to health, in T2DM. Study visits lasted a single session of roughly 2.5-4 hours followed by 10 days of at-home monitoring. The released data contain no protected health information; information related to the sex and race/ethnicity of participants and to medications used has been removed from this public release.

  • Response
    The ability to understand and affect the course of complex, multi-organ diseases such as T2DM has been limited by a lack of well-designed, high quality, large, and inclusive multimodal datasets.
  • Response
    A major barrier has been the lack of diverse datasets that are AI ready for training AI models; the AI-READI dataset is intended to fill this gap.
  • Response
    Existing large datasets for AI/ML training have inherent limitations of demographic imbalance; a meta-epidemiological review of T2DM studies published from 2000 to 2020 found that samples lacked racial and ethnic diversity, and this collection is an important step forward in addressing those demographic inequities in research.
  • Missing Data Patterns
    Not all modalities are available for all participants; within survey data, skipped questions and incomplete responses occur. Sex, race, ethnicity and medications used are absent for every participant in this release, having been removed before publication.
    Missing Data Causes
    Some participants elected not to participate in some study elements. In a few cases the data collection device did not have any stored results or was returned too late to retrieve the results, for example because the battery died and data was lost. In a few cases there may have been a data collision at some point in the process and data has been lost. Wearable data are additionally affected by improper use and by technical failure such as battery failure or system malfunction, and the Garmin Vivosmart 5 had to be recharged every 2-3 days, leaving gaps. Imaging data are affected by patient uncooperation, image noise, system malfunction and data transfer failures, and manually operated devices sometimes led to a modality being skipped.
    Handling Strategy
    Missing data that could be directly filled from other portions of an individual's record were filled during data cleaning under the guidance and approval of the site principal investigator; where there was no logical or appropriate way to correct a value, the site principal investigator decided whether the value should be removed. No imputation of missing modality data is described, and no instances were excluded at the time of preprocessing.
  • Relationship Details
    All instances are part of the same prospective data generation project (AI-READI), and there is currently only one visit per participant. Within the release, every data type, modality and device directory contains one folder per participant named with the participant's study ID, so a participant's records can be linked across modalities.
  • Split Details
    The release includes a recommended split into training (1576 participants), validation (352) and test (352) sets, assigned per participant in participants.tsv. Because sex, race and ethnicity are not released with the public version, the project team prepared splits in 70%/15%/15% proportions in which the validation and test sets are balanced as well as possible for sex, race/ethnicity and diabetes status (no diabetes, prediabetes/lifestyle controlled, oral medication controlled, and insulin controlled). Mean age is 61.1 ± 11.3 years in the training set, 60.3 ± 10.8 in validation and 60.5 ± 11.4 in test. The recommended split is new in version 3.0.0 and is intended to reduce disparities in models developed using this dataset.
  • Anomaly Details
    In survey data, skipped questions or incomplete responses are expected.
  • Anomaly Details
    In wearable data, improper use and technical failure such as battery failure or system malfunction are expected. The Garmin Vivosmart 5 had to be charged every 2-3 days, so there are gaps in activity data collection.
  • Anomaly Details
    In imaging data, patient uncooperation, noise that may obscure the images, technical failure such as system malfunction, and data transfer failures are expected.
  • Anomaly Details
    Undilated handheld fundus photography with the Optomed Aurora is challenging because the pupil is not dilated and the camera is handheld, so image quality varies from patient to patient and within the same patient.
  • Anomaly Details
    FLIO acquisition takes longer than a standard Spectralis OCT and exposes participants to uncomfortable levels of light, so it is performed last in the visit and participants may not be at their best possible compliance; the device cannot tolerate more than one centimeter of movement during acquisition.
  • Anomaly Details
    Devices operated manually rather than automatically (for example the Heidelberg Spectralis) can raise quality issues that led to skipping a modality and missing data.

  • Mechanism Details

  • Mechanism Details
    Blood pressure and heart rate measured twice with automatic oscillometric devices: OMRON HEM 907XL, Medline MDS4001 Automatic Digital Blood Pressure Monitor, or Welch Allyn 6000 series vital signs monitor with a FlexiPort reusable cuff.
  • Mechanism Details
    10 g monofilament testing of touch perception at three locations on each foot for peripheral neuropathy, with results recorded on an iPad, laptop or paper questionnaire and entered into REDCap.
  • Mechanism Details

  • Mechanism Details

  • Mechanism Details

  • Mechanism Details
    Autorefraction with the Topcon KR 800 auto keratometer/refractor, and lensometry with the NIDEK LM-600P, Topcon CL-200 or Topcon CL-300 lensometers.
  • Mechanism Details
    Undilated handheld color fundus photography with the Optomed Aurora IQ, a non-mydriatic handheld camera with a 50 degree field of view and 5 Mpix sensor.
  • Mechanism Details
    Dilated widefield TrueColor confocal fundus imaging with the iCare EIDON, in fully automated mode, capturing single-field central infrared and fundus autofluorescence images and a smart horizontal mosaic.
  • Mechanism Details
    Optical coherence tomography and OCT angiography with the Heidelberg Spectralis HRA+OCT using the ONH-RC, PPole-H and macular OCTA protocols.
  • Mechanism Details
    Fluorescence lifetime imaging ophthalmoscopy with the Heidelberg FLIO, macula-centered, acquired last in the visit.
  • Mechanism Details
    Swept-source OCT and OCTA with the Topcon DRI OCT Triton, using 12.0 x 12.0 mm and 6.0 x 6.0 mm OCTA and 12.0 x 9.0 x 6.0 mm 3D horizontal and radial scan modes.
  • Mechanism Details
    OCT, OCTA and color fundus photography with the Topcon Maestro2 3D OCT-1 robotic system, using 12.0 x 9.0 mm widefield, 6.0 x 6.0 mm 3D macula and 6.0 x 6.0 mm OCTA (50 kHz) protocols.
  • Mechanism Details

  • Mechanism Details
    Dexcom G6 real-time integrated continuous glucose monitoring system, worn continuously for 10 days after the visit, capturing readings every five minutes; participants were masked to the results during the monitoring period.
  • Mechanism Details
    Garmin Vivosmart 5 fitness activity tracker, worn for 10 consecutive days on the participant's chosen wrist, with the wrist worn and the dominant hand recorded on a returned data sheet; sampling frequency 5 seconds.
  • Mechanism Details

    Strategies
    Non-probability sampling. A contact pool was identified for each recruitment wave by screening electronic health records at each site using ICD-10 codes (R73.09 for pre-diabetes and E11.X for T2DM) and demographic data; invitations were mailed and emailed with personalized links into an online REDCap recruitment interface. Recruitment proceeded in waves whose composition and size were influenced by the observed participation characteristics of race/ethnicity, gender, severity of T2DM and site, with under- and oversampling of groups as needed.
    Source Data
    All patients aged 40 years or older who had a medical encounter within each health system site (University of Alabama at Birmingham, University of California San Diego, University of Washington).
    Why Not Representative
    Rather than targeting the demographic distribution of the US population, the study is triple-balanced by recruiting four race/ethnic groups (Asian, black, white, Hispanic), four categories of T2DM severity and biological males and females in equal proportions, because building balanced datasets is critical for the development of unbiased machine learning models.
    Notes
    The healthsheet states that the dataset contains all possible instances, that is data from all participants enrolled by the data cut-off, and answers "N/A" to the question about sampling strategy for a sample of a larger set; the study itself is recorded as a non-probability sample of the health-system population described above.

    Access Details
    The raw data is saved and expected to be preserved by the AI-READI project at least for the duration of the project, but is not anticipated to be shared outside the project team at present because it has not been mapped to standardized terminologies and may accidentally include personal health information or personally identifiable information, for example in free text fields; there is a possibility that raw data may be included in future releases of the controlled access dataset.
  • Cleaning Details
    Quality control measures were applied at the time of data entry and acquisition: clinical data outside expected minimum/maximum ranges were flagged in REDCap and surfaced in reports viewed by clinical research coordinators and data managers, who examined participant records to determine whether an error was likely. Data were checked, and edited where errors were detected, for credibility against prespecified reasonable ranges, incorrect flow through prescribed skip patterns, missing data that could be filled directly from other portions of an individual's record, and the omission or duplication of records. Editing was done only under the guidance and approval of the site principal investigator; where there was no logical or appropriate way to correct a value, the site PI decided whether it should be removed.
  • Preprocessing Details
    After data were sent from the study sites to the central project team, all data were mapped to standardized terminologies where possible, including the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP) Common Data Model for clinical data and DICOM for medical imaging; proprietary device exports were transformed into standard model formats before upload to FAIRhub, with Topcon .fda and FLIO .sdt files converted to DICOM and Garmin .FIT files converted to the Open mHealth standard. Processing for this release combined automated and custom steps, in contrast with the custom/ad hoc processing of version 1.0.0.
  • Preprocessing Details
    No instances were excluded at the time of preprocessing; exclusions arise only from the study eligibility criteria (age 40 or older, able to consent, able to read and speak English, not pregnant, no gestational diabetes, no type 1 diabetes).
