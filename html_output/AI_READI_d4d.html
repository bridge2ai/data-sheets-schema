Response
The dataset was created to enable future generations of artificial intelligence/machine learning (AI/ML) research to provide critical insights into type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), including salutogenic pathways to return to health.
The AI-READI project also aims to establish standards, best practices and guidelines for the collection, preparation and sharing of data for AI/ML, as a model for future efforts to develop other health datasets.

doi:10.60775/fairhub.3
AI-READI dataset
Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
Version 3.0.0 of the AI-READI dataset, the public release distributed through the FAIRhub platform. It contains data from 2280 participants collected between July 19, 2023 and May 1, 2025 from individuals with and without type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), harmonized across 3 data collection sites. Multiple modalities are included for each participant: survey data, clinical data, retinal imaging data, environmental sensor data, continuous glucose monitor data, and wearable activity monitor data, encompassing tabular data, imaging data, and physiological signal/waveform data, with no unstructured text data. The data contain no protected health information; information related to the sex and race/ethnicity of the participants, as well as medication used, has been removed. The dataset contains 356,343 files and is around 3.82 TB in size. Data are organized at the root level into one directory per datatype; within each datatype folder there is one folder per modality, within each modality folder one folder per device, and within each device folder one folder per participant.

Response
The current understanding of T2DM, especially in the context of salutogenesis, is limited; AI-based approaches may help, but a key issue is the lack of data ready for training AI models. The AI-READI dataset is intended to fill this gap.
To address inherent limitations of many existing large datasets for AI/ML training, the cohort is designed to be balanced in race/ethnicity, T2DM severity and biological sex.
The ability to understand and affect the course of complex, multi-organ diseases such as T2DM has been limited by a lack of well-designed, high quality, and large multimodal datasets.
  • Relationship Details
    All instances are part of the same prospective data generation project (AI-READI). There is currently only one visit per participant.
  • Split Details
    The current version comes with recommended data splits. Because sex, race, and ethnicity data are not released with the public version, the project team prepared splits in proportions 70%/15%/15% for training/validation/testing, where the validation and test sets are balanced as well as possible for sex, race/ethnicity and diabetes status. The README summary gives 1576 participants in the training set, 352 in the validation set and 352 in the test set, 2280 in total. The suggested split is included in the participants.tsv file.
Anomaly Details
In survey data, skipped questions or incomplete responses are expected.
In data from wearables, improper use and technical failure such as battery failure or system malfunction are expected.
In imaging data, patient uncooperation, noise that may obscure the images, technical failure such as system malfunction, and data transfer failures are expected.
  • Missing Data Patterns
    Marijuana use was not assessed for participants living in Alabama.
    Missing Data Causes
    The protocol publication states it was not assessed there because marijuana is an illegal substance in Alabama.
  • Bias Type
    selection_bias
    Bias Description
    Participants are volunteers; the protocol publication states there is therefore selection bias known as volunteer bias, which may limit the generalizability of the results among those who are not represented in the study population.
Limitation DescriptionLimitation Type
Eligible participants were required to speak, read and understand English; the protocol publication states this may have created challenges in recruiting Hispanic and/or Asian persons.
The overall enrollment goal is a balanced distribution between groups, but as enrollment is ongoing the periodic data releases may not have achieved balanced distribution across groups.
Recruitment was predominantly urban and hospital-based, which may not fully capture diverse cultural and socioeconomic backgrounds; the healthsheet names this among the factors influencing the generalization of derived models.
The study cohort may not provide a comprehensive representation of the population, as it does not include other races/ethnicities such as Pacific Islanders and Native Americans.

    Balanced recruitment design
    Strategies
    Rather than targeting the demographic distribution of the US population, the study is designed to be triple-balanced by recruiting in equal proportions four race/ethnic groups (Asian, Black, white, Hispanic), four categories of T2DM (no diabetes, pre-diabetes/lifestyle- controlled diabetes, diabetes treated with oral or non-insulin- injectable medications, and insulin-controlled diabetes) and biological males and females. The FAIRhub study description gives an anticipated enrollment of 4000 and states the aim of recruiting approximately 1000 patients into each of the four groups.

  • Name
    Recruitment in waves from screened health records
    Strategies
    Recruitment pools were identified by screening electronic health records for diabetes and prediabetes ICD-10 codes, and participants are recruited in waves whose composition and size are adjusted, by under- and oversampling groups as needed, according to the observed participation by race/ethnicity, gender, severity of T2DM and site.
    Description
    The IRB protocol application describes the plan: the three sites pool lists of potentially eligible patients, the University of Alabama at Birmingham selects a stratified sample of potential participants based on the sampling scheme, and the sites prioritize different racial/ethnic groups according to local demographics.
    Source Caveats
    The healthsheet says the health-record screen covered patients with an encounter within the past 2 years; the protocol publication gives the study base as encounters between 2020 and 2025.

Consent Details
Informed consent to participate was required before participation in any part of the protocol, including questionnaires. Potential participants were given the option to read all consent documentation electronically (e-consent) before their visit and give their consent with an electronic signature. The IRB protocol application states that consent for secondary use, banking and future sharing is obtained through language included in the consent form, and that the consent form begins with a table clarifying which information will be publicly available and which would require an application and approval process.
The FAIRhub metadata record the consent type as "ConsentSpecifiedNotElsewhereCategorised" and state that the public version of the dataset can only be used for type 2 diabetes related research, while a private version will allow for more generic use.
Notification Details
Each individual was aware of the data collection, as this was not passive data collection or secondary use of existing data but active data collection directly from participants.
Results are returned to participants: results from heart rate, blood pressure and visual acuity assessments are provided on an exam card before they leave the visit, a Dexcom report is provided by encrypted email after the at-home monitoring period, and laboratory test results are sent at the time of the yearly data release.

Preprocessing Details
All participant images are exported from imaging devices in their raw format, and some images required conversion to a DICOM standard format prior to upload.
Once data were sent from each of the study sites to the central project team, all data were mapped to standardized terminologies when possible, such as the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP) Common Data Model for clinical data and the Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) standard for imaging data.
Cleaning Details
The Data Manager at each data site is responsible for the quality control of all data before uploading to FAIRhub.
Quality control at the time of data entry and acquisition: clinical data outside of expected min/max ranges were flagged in REDCap; data managers and clinical research coordinators examined participant records for credibility (range checks), incorrect flow through prescribed skip patterns, missing data that can be directly filled from other portions of an individual's record, and the omission or duplication of records. Editing was only done under the guidance and approval of the site PI.
The dataset has not undergone any formal external audits; it has been reviewed internally by AI-READI team members for quality checks and to ensure that no personally identifiable information was accidentally included.
  • Raw Data Details
    The raw data is saved and expected to be preserved by the AI-READI project at least for the duration of the project, but is not anticipated to be shared outside the project team at the time of the healthsheet, because it has not been mapped to standardized terminologies and may accidentally include personal health information or personally identifiable information. The healthsheet notes a possibility that raw data may be included in future releases of the controlled access dataset.

Version Details
The first version contains data from 204 participants from the pilot data collection phase; the second version contains additional data from 863 participants, for a total of 1067 participants; this third version contains a total of 2280 participants. The FAIRhub page and the documentation for version 2.0.0 state that that version is no longer accessible and refer to the latest version. The data fields and types are largely the same as in the prior version; the Snellen visual acuity variables were dropped in this version, so that only logMAR visual acuity measurements are available.
