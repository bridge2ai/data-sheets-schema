Bridge2AI-Voice
Bridge2AI-Voice: An ethically-sourced, diverse voice dataset linked to health information
Bridge2AI-Voice is an ethically sourced, multi-site dataset of voice, speech, breathing and cough data linked to demographic, clinical and validated-questionnaire information, built to support research into voice as a biomarker of health. Version 3.1.0 covers 833 adult participants recruited at five North American sites and selected for conditions that manifest within the voice waveform: voice disorders, neurological and neurodegenerative disorders, mood and psychiatric disorders and respiratory disorders, alongside control participants. Participants completed a standardized protocol of acoustic tasks on a tablet application, with a headset where possible, together with demographic, confounder, disease-specific and validated-questionnaire instruments; clinical diagnoses were validated by clinicians against site gold standards. The public release distributes data considered low risk — spectrograms, Mel spectrograms, MFCCs, pitch and periodicity traces, Speech Articulatory Coding articulatory estimates, phonetic posteriorgrams, openSMILE, Praat and parselmouth static features, per-recording audio quality metrics, per-recording metadata and phenotype tables — rather than the original waveforms. Raw audio is disseminated separately, under controlled access only.

  • Preprocessing Details
    Raw audio was standardized by converting to monaural and resampling to 16 kHz with a Butterworth anti-aliasing filter applied.
  • Preprocessing Details
    Spectrograms were computed from the standardized audio using the short-time Fast Fourier Transform with a 25 ms window size, 10 ms hop length and a 400-point FFT, then downsampled by a factor of two in the time domain. Mel spectrograms use the same parameters with 60 Mel bins, and 60 Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients were extracted from the spectrograms.
  • Preprocessing Details
    Articulatory features were generated with the Speech Articulatory Coding (sparc) package, giving kinematic traces of vocal tract articulators and source features together with measures of loudness, periodicity and pitch, all gathered at 50 Hz.
  • Preprocessing Details
    Phonetic posteriorgrams, a time-varying categorical distribution over acoustic units of speech at 100 Hz across 40 phoneme categories, were generated with the ppgs package.
  • Preprocessing Details
    Acoustic features were extracted with openSMILE, and phonetic and prosodic features — measures of fundamental frequency, formants and voice quality — were computed with Parselmouth and Praat.
  • Preprocessing Details
    Transcriptions were generated using OpenAI's Whisper Large model.
  • Cleaning Details
    An audit protocol was applied: generate missingness tables; check distributions and outliers; check categorical responses against the schema; run quality control metrics for audio tasks; and for waveforms check the amount of silence and the duration, and for speech check the produced speech relative to the intended passage. All processing was performed with the b2aiprep and SenseLab toolkits.
  • Cleaning Details
    Audio quality control metrics derived from the raw recordings are released alongside the features as audio_quality_metrics.tsv, one row per audio recording.
  • Labeling Details
    Gold standard diagnostic labels were taken from clinical assessment. At each site a local clinician provided the diagnosis; for the psychiatric disorders cohort the label was determined from the participant's electronic health record or an active prescription.

  • Missing Data Patterns

Method
HIPAA Safe Harbor, supplemented by removal of indirect identifiers and of non-identifying but highly sensitive fields, and by withholding of all raw audio.
Identifiers Removed
HIPAA Safe Harbor identifiers, including names, geographic locators, date information at a resolution finer than years, phone and fax numbers, email addresses, IP addresses, Social Security numbers, medical record numbers, health plan beneficiary numbers, device identifiers, license numbers, account numbers, vehicle identifiers, website URLs, full face photos, biometric identifiers and any unique identifiers; state and province, with country of data collection retained; select geographic or demographic identifiers and some information about household composition or cultural identity; and non-identifying but highly sensitive elements such as household income, mental health status and traumatic life experiences.
Deidentification Details
All raw voice data was removed because it has the potential to cause individual re-identification or to be used for illicit or unauthorized purposes, and only extracted features are released. Raw audio transcripts were reviewed and any recordings containing potentially identifying information or external voices were removed from the release; spectrograms and similar features were excluded where the audio contained free speech, while static and other features that do not encode the sensitive information were retained. Sensitive fields flagged in the REDCap data dictionary were removed. K-anonymization with k of two or more is not achieved.
