Flagship Dataset of Type 2 Diabetes from the AI-READI Project
The AI-READI flagship dataset is a multimodal, AI-ready dataset collected from 2,280 adults aged 40 and older with and without type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), harmonized across three United States data collection sites (University of Alabama at Birmingham, University of California San Diego, and University of Washington). Version 3.0.0 contains data collected between 19 July 2023 and 1 May 2025 spanning survey data, clinical data mapped to the OMOP Common Data Model, 12-lead electrocardiography in WFDB, retinal photography, optical coherence tomography, optical coherence tomography angiography and fluorescence lifetime imaging ophthalmoscopy in DICOM, home environmental sensing, continuous glucose monitoring and wearable activity monitoring. Recruitment sampling aimed at an approximately equal distribution of participants across race/ethnicity, sex and diabetes severity (no diabetes; pre-diabetes or lifestyle-controlled; oral or non-insulin-injectable medication controlled; insulin controlled) so that downstream AI/ML analyses are feasible in ways they are not with claims or electronic health record data. The stated goal is to better understand salutogenesis, the pathway from disease to health, in T2DM. This public version contains no protected health information; information related to the sex and race/ethnicity of participants and medications used has also been removed and is held in a controlled-access version obtainable under a data use agreement.

  • Sample

    Random Sampling
    false
    Representative Sample
    false
    Strategies
    Participants were recruited in waves to facilitate efficient sampling of the study base, with the composition and size of each wave influenced by the observed participation characteristics of race/ethnicity, gender, severity of T2DM and site; composition was monitored and adjusted by under- and oversampling groups as needed. For each wave a contact pool was identified by screening electronic health records at each site for ICD-10 diagnosis codes R73.09 and E11.X, with patients without diabetes identified as those without encounters with these codes. Recruitment aimed at approximately equal proportions of four race/ethnic groups, four categories of T2DM severity and biological males and females, rather than at matching the demographic distribution of the US population. The study is registered with a sampling method of "Non-Probability Sample" and an observational cohort, cross-sectional design.
    Source Data
    All patients aged 40 years or older who had a medical encounter within each health system site (University of Alabama at Birmingham, University of California San Diego, University of Washington) between 2020 and 2025.
    Why Not Representative
    The cohort is deliberately balanced rather than demographically representative; recruitment is predominantly urban and hospital-based; participants are volunteers; and races and ethnicities such as Pacific Islanders and Native Americans are not included.
    Source Caveats
    The bundle is internally inconsistent on whether the dataset is a sample. The healthsheet answers that "The dataset contains all possible instances", meaning all participants enrolled within the collection period, and answers "N/A" to the question of the sampling strategy used to draw the dataset from a larger set. Against this, the same-tier FAIRhub study description registers the samplingMethod as "Non-Probability Sample", and the protocol publication describes a defined study base (all patients aged 40 or older with an encounter at the three health systems between 2020 and 2025) from which wave-based, EHR-screened, balance-adjusted contact pools were drawn. `is_sample` is set true on the strength of the registered sampling method and the documented wave sampling; the healthsheet's "all possible instances" reading is best understood as a statement about completeness within the release window rather than about the relationship to the study base.
  • Cleaning Details

Editing was done only under the guidance and approval of the site principal investigator; where there was no logical or appropriate way to correct the data, the site principal investigator reviewed the values and decided whether they should be removed.
