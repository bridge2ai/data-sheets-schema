CHoRUS Dataset
Bridge2AI: Patient-Focused Collaborative Hospital Repository Uniting Standards (CHoRUS) for Equitable AI
CHoRUS is a multicenter, multimodal, high-resolution, controlled-access critical care dataset produced by the Patient-Focused Collaborative Hospital Repository Uniting Standards (CHoRUS) for Equitable AI project, one of four data generation projects in the NIH-funded Bridge2AI consortium. Data are collected retrospectively from the clinical systems of 14 data contributing hospitals within a collaboration spanning 20 academic centers, and are harmonized to unified standards: structured electronic health record data are standardized to the OMOP Common Data Model, imaging from PACS is held as DICOM, bedside monitor telemetry as WFDB waveforms, hospital EEG as EDF+ and Persyst, and clinical notes are extracted and tokenized using the OHNLP toolkit. The current released dataset reported by the project website comprises 50,000 patient admissions from ICU, PICU and NICU settings, 1.6 billion rows of EHR OMOP data, 7,642 admissions with radiology data, and 23 Tb of waveform data; the anticipated final dataset covers 100,000 patient admissions across 9 data modalities. All data types are under controlled access. As presented in September 2025, OMOP and telemetry data reside in a cloud enclave, clinical notes are stored locally at the contributing sites except for tokens, imaging de-identification was in process for a larger cohort, and EEG extraction was in process. Data acquisition, standardization and modality expansion continue within the NIH project period 2022-09-01 through 2026-11-30.

  • Task Details
    Beyond model development, the data serve as a teaching corpus: canonical Jupyter notebooks, workshops on the OHDSI tool stack and the OHDSI/OMOP common data model, and didactics on generative AI and specific use cases are delivered on the Bridge2AI AI/ML for Clinical Care Collaborative Cloud.
  • Impact Details
    The project anticipates that the dataset will catalyze biomedical research and expertise needed to promote patient-focused deployment of AI in acute and critical care, and that the holdout set will aid marketplace adoption of AI-developed models.
  • Impact Details
    Because the data derive from critically ill patients, the project pairs future use with privacy safeguards: transformations that limit re-identification, community-facing ethics focus groups on what data is appropriate for public sharing, and analysis of the legal and regulatory landscape to manage privacy and bias while accounting for social determinants of health.
